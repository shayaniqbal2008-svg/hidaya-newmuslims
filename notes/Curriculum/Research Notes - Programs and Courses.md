# Research Notes: Existing Programs and Courses for New Muslims

Research area B: existing in-person, online, and hybrid programs for converts. Everything here is summarized from free public pages, with URLs given. Quotes are short (under 15 words) and attributed. "WebFetch" means the page was fetched and read. "Search snippet" means only the search-engine summary was seen, so confidence is lower. Nothing here is a religious ruling. It only records what programs teach and in what order.

> Status: complete (2026-10-05). 20 program profiles plus brief notes on AlMaghrib, Qalam, Cambridge, MCN, and the apps table.

## Program profiles

### Ta'leef Collective: Convert Care / Newcomers
- **Organization & people:** Nonprofit started in 2002 as Zaytuna Institute's outreach program and independent since 2005 ([SeekersGuidance article](https://seekersguidance.org/articles/general-artices/taleef-collective-an-amazing-project/)). Centers in Fremont, CA and Chicago, IL. The Convert Care staff named on the archived site are Mahdy Amine, Mike Swies, and Alaa Suliman. Teachers include Usama Canon (founder), Dr. Will Caldwell, and others. Its tradition is traditional Sunni and close to Zaytuna and Hamza Yusuf's network. Motto: "Come as you are to Islam as it is."
- **Format:** Hybrid. In-person centers, weekly Zoom classes, one-on-one consultation, peer community events, and an annual retreat.
- **Length & cadence:** Ongoing with no fixed end. The parts are:
  - "Being Muslim": a weekly online class (Wednesday or Friday 5:30pm PT has been listed)
  - "Establishing the Prayer": a quarterly 2-hour in-person workshop in the Bay Area
  - "Introducing Islam": a quarterly 2-hour session in Chicago
  - "Welcome to the Fold": recurring peer and social gatherings
  - Annual Newcomers' Retreat: 4 nights and 5 days, held at Zaytuna College in Berkeley
- **Cost:** The archived Convert Care page says it is free. Retreat pricing is not shown.
- **Intended audience:** Seekers before shahada and new Muslims. Ta'leef also serves young Muslims who are "emerging" or re-engaging.
- **Topics in order:** There is no published sequenced syllabus. The "Being Muslim" class follows Asad Tarsin's book *Being Muslim: A Practical Guide*. According to the event listing, its topics go roughly in this order:
  1. Introduction to the vision and spirit of Islam
  2. Introduction to the Qur'an
  3. Prayer and fasting
  4. Morality in everyday life / good character
  5. Destiny and fate
  6. Life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
  - Separately, the "Establishing the Prayer" workshop covers practicing prayer, the verbal parts of prayer, and the why and how of salah.
  - Retreat topics: Being Muslim, the life of the Prophet, and self-care and mindfulness.
- **Pacing & structure:** Contact usually starts with a one-on-one consultation. The person then joins the rolling weekly class (no cohort start date) plus quarterly hands-on prayer workshops and monthly or periodic "Welcome to the Fold" community events. Chicago also has monthly "Living Right" discussions with Usama Canon ([Chicago Muslim Convert](https://www.chicagomuslimconvert.com/learning-opportunities.html)).
- **Tone / philosophy:** Belonging first and "meet people where they are." The archive calls it a "full-scale system" running from first interest to "sustainable conversion and holistic practice." Ta'leef puts heavy weight on community, arts and culture, and making the space feel "like family" (participant testimonial).
- **Retention or outcome claims:** The archived page says "Welcome to the Fold" has "assisted over 500 people in embracing Islam." No completion or retention data is published.
- **Strengths:** The best-known US convert-care brand. It pairs a strong community and belonging model with a real book-based curriculum (*Being Muslim*) and a hands-on prayer workshop. The retreat format builds deep bonds.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** No public syllabus or progress milestones. The new site is partly under construction, and most detail sits on archive.taleefcollective.org. The in-person programs reach only two metros. No outcome data.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch of the archived Convert Care page, the live newcomers-events page (June 2022 listings), and the newcomers page. Topic list comes from a search snippet of the Ta'leef event page, because the live event URL returned 404.
- **URL(s):** https://archive.taleefcollective.org/convert-care/ ; https://www.taleefcollective.org/newcomers ; https://www.taleefcollective.org/newcomers-events ; https://www.taleefcollective.org/newcomers-events/event-two-zmf5c
- **Confidence:** Medium. Program structure is high. The topic order is inferred from a snippet and the book.

#### Where does "20,000 conversions/year" come from?
- The Islamic Seminary of America's Converts page credits the figure to Ta'leef ("According to Ta'leef Collective… over 20,000 Americans convert", [islamicseminary.us/converts](https://islamicseminary.us/converts/)). In the same passage it says many converts leave within about two years. I found no primary Ta'leef page that gives a method behind the number. The current site and the archived Convert Care page don't state it (WebFetch, 2026-10-05).
- The figure is older than Ta'leef's use of it. A 2011 Religion News Service / Huffington Post piece said "observers estimate that as many as 20,000 Americans convert" each year, with no named study (cited via [Wikipedia: Conversion to Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Islam), search snippet). The phrase is commonly traced further back to estimates by Ihsan Bagby and US mosque studies from around 2000–2001. Not verified.
- Pew (2017) is more defensible: about 21–24% of US Muslim adults are converts. That figure measures stock, not an annual flow.
- **Bottom line:** "20,000/year" is a community estimate passed from source to source (observers → RNS/HuffPost → Ta'leef → ISA). It is not a measured statistic. Confidence that no rigorous primary source exists: medium. Pitch materials should cite it as "commonly cited estimate."

- **Organization & people:** The Islamic Seminary of America (Richardson/Dallas area, TX). Accredited-track Islamic studies seminary offering an MA in Islamic Studies, a Master of Divinity (chaplaincy), and certificates.
- **Format:** Online and in-person graduate programs. There is no dedicated convert program.
- **Length & cadence:** N/A for converts.
- **Cost:** N/A (degree tuition only).
- **Intended audience:** Its "Converts" page is an advocacy and awareness article written for community leaders.
- **Topics in order:** No convert syllabus is offered. The article recommends four kinds of support: (1) mentoring programs, (2) support networks, (3) educational materials, and (4) social and family events.
- **Pacing & structure:** N/A.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Problem framing. It says "there is no standardized introductory curriculum" for US converts and cites ISPU findings that converts get "criticized, frowned upon, or rudely corrected" at mosques.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** Repeats Ta'leef's 20,000/year figure and says many leave within two years.
- **Strengths:** A ready-made, citable statement of the exact gap our product targets.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Diagnoses the problem but offers no curriculum.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch.
- **URL(s):** https://islamicseminary.us/converts/
- **Confidence:** High for what the page says. Its statistics are second-hand.

### New Muslim Academy (NMA)
- **Organization & people:** Nonprofit with an English-language online academy. Neither the site nor the FAQ names its operator or location. A Gambian university news post covers it ([IOU news](https://iou.edu.gm/iou_news/new-muslim-academy-free-online-learning-program-for-reverts-to-islam/)), which suggests links to the Islamic Online University / Bilal Philips ecosystem. Not verified.
- **Format:** Online and self-paced, with optional live sessions, forums, and one-on-one "consulting."
- **Length & cadence:** Each course takes about one month. Enrollment runs in cycles and courses can be retaken.
- **Cost:** Free. The site says courses "are (and will always be) free," including mentoring and live sessions.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims worldwide who speak English.
- **Topics in order:** The site lists two tiers in this order. Advanced courses require finishing the introductory tier.
  - Introductory: 1. Messenger of Mercy (life of the Prophet) 2. Health & Happiness 3. Muslim Relationships 4. Success in Wealth 5. Muslims at Home 6. Muslims at Work 7. Salah: Watch & See How It's Done
  - Advanced: 8. Introduction to Islam 9. Faith & Belief (aqeedah) 10. Worship (fiqh of worship)
  - Topic pages also exist for "Allah" and "Eid."
- **Pacing & structure:** Learners study alone and set their own schedule. Materials include PDFs and video lectures, plus student forums and "competitions." Teachers are said to be available for questions.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Starts with life skills and the Prophet's character (relationships, work, wealth, wellbeing) and puts creed and fiqh in the advanced tier. That ordering is unusual and worth noting.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** None published.
- **Strengths:** Free, clearly tiered, and practical. Its "Muslims at Home / at Work" modules address daily-life integration, which most programs skip.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Lessons per course and instructors aren't public. Operator transparency is low. No outcome data. The site looks dated.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch of the course list and FAQ.
- **URL(s):** https://www.newmuslimacademy.org/our-courses/ ; https://www.newmuslimacademy.org/faq-section/
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### iERA New Muslim Programme (UK)
- **Organization & people:** iERA (Islamic Education and Research Academy), a UK dawah organization based in London. Instructors for the weekly class are not named.
- **Format:** A live online weekly class via GoToWebinar, with lecture plus interactive discussion. iERA's London base has also run an in-person Tuesday class. Other offerings are a one-to-one mentoring program, a welcome gift box, a learning portal (newmuslim.iera.org), a webinar series, and consultation calls.
- **Length & cadence:** 14 sessions, held weekly on Sundays (so about 3.5 months).
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims. The mentoring program is for those aged 18 and over.
- **Topics in order:** (session titles from the class page)
  1. Welcome to the course
  2. Setting the foundations
  3. The Guidance
  4. Establishing the foundations
  5. The three categories of tawheed
  6. Five pillars: Shahada (part 1)
  7. Five pillars: Shahada (part 2)
  8. Five pillars: Salah
  9. Five pillars: Charity
  10. Five pillars: Fasting
  11. Five pillars: Pilgrimage
  12. Challenges of life
  13. Protecting your faith
  14. Preparing to meet your Lord
- **Pacing & structure:** One cohort session per week. Mentoring is a separate application track, in which a mentor reviews the mentee's "action plans" and shares learning materials. No homework is described for the class.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Creed first (tawheed taxonomy in week 5), then the pillars, then resilience. It reflects a Salafi-leaning dawah tradition.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** None found on these pages.
- **Strengths:** A clean public 14-step sequence. It ends with "challenges of life" and "protecting your faith," which touch retention. It also combines class, mentor, and gift box.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Prayer gets one session even though it's the hardest practical skill. Nothing covers family, community integration, or identity. Instructors aren't listed.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch.
- **URL(s):** https://iera.org/new-muslim/weekly-class/ ; https://newmuslim.iera.org/new-muslim-mentor/
- **Confidence:** High for the session list. Medium for other details.

### Embrace (ICNA): "Islam: Step by Step Fundamentals" and Islam 101
- **Organization & people:** Embrace is a project of **ICNA (Islamic Circle of North America)**, not ISNA. The brief said ISNA, but the sources consistently say ICNA ([icna.org/embrace](https://icna.org/embrace/), [icnadallas.org/embrace](https://icnadallas.org/embrace/)). It was founded in Dallas in 2017 (its own site says 2018) and describes itself as "for reverts by reverts." Converts lead it. National secretary Kenneth Misurella is quoted in the AboutIslam article. By 2020 it had chapters in Dallas, Houston, Connecticut, Pennsylvania, and Sacramento, and its site also mentions Florida. Yaqeen Institute is listed as a partner.
- **Format:** Hybrid. In-person classes in Dallas, virtual and in-person halaqas, a "Sister's Virtual Coffee/Tea Chat," and recorded class videos.
- **Length & cadence:** There are two documented courses:
  - **Islam 101:** 8 weeks, taught by a "convert Imam" with 20+ years of experience teaching converts.
  - **Step by Step Fundamentals:** 14 weekly 2-hour sessions over about 3 months, run seasonally.
  - Also weekly halaqas, a daily Qur'an reading program, and annual Ramadan and Eid programs.
- **Cost:** Not stated. Likely free.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims, with an emphasis on American converts. It aims to present Islam "without cultural biases" (Islam 101 page).
- **Topics in order:** (Step by Step. Weeks 1–9 come from the class page, and weeks 10–14 were on a second page that wasn't readable.)
  1. Setting the foundations
  2. The Guidance
  3. Establishing the foundations
  4. The three categories of tawheed
  5. Rulings on some common practices
  6. The second part of the shahadah
  7. Second pillar: Prayer (salah)
  8. Third pillar: Zakat
  9. Fourth pillar: Fasting
  10–14. Not visible. Likely Hajj, then life challenges, protecting faith, and the hereafter, if it follows the iERA sequence (see note below).
  - Islam 101 (8 weeks) covers core beliefs, the pillars, and faith, plus "step-by-step purification and prayer" and spiritual excellence. No weekly order is public.
- **Pacing & structure:** A cohort meets weekly and recordings are posted. Halaqas and social chats run alongside. There's a "New Muslim Starter Pack" and a giveaway program for clothing, books, and Qur'ans.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Retention-focused. The AboutIslam article quotes the goal to "address the high turnover of converts leaving Islam." Embrace also runs **Convert Sensitivity Training** for Muslim communities, which is a B2B-style offering to masjids.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** 300–350 active participants across chapters and 9,000+ Facebook followers (AboutIslam, about 2020). It cites "seven out of 10 converts" leaving, which traces to Imam Luqman Ahmad's 2010 blog estimate ([imamluqman.com](https://imamluqman.com/2010/01/13/seven-out-of-every-ten-converts-leave-islam-by-imam-luqman-ahmad/)), not to research. A search snippet also ties Embrace to a Pew "net conversion rate of 0%" claim. Pew has reported that roughly as many Americans leave Islam as join it.
- **Strengths:** Convert-led, multi-city, and on a national org's platform. It has a public week-by-week syllabus, and its sensitivity training points to demand from masjids.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** The second half of the syllabus isn't visible. Instructors aren't named on the Step by Step page. Outcome data is thin.
- **Note: shared curriculum.** Embrace's Step by Step titles match iERA's 14-session weekly class almost word for word, from "Setting the Foundations" and "The Guidance" to "3 Categories of Tawheed." That points to a common source text (probably an iERA or similar new-Muslim syllabus). So at least one convert curriculum is already being reused across organizations, and a standard curriculum could spread the same way.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch of embracereverts.org (home, Islam 101 page, Step by Step page) and the AboutIslam article. Search snippets for ICNA pages.
- **URL(s):** https://embracereverts.org/ ; https://embracereverts.org/islam-step-by-step-fundamentals-class/ ; https://embracereverts.org/project/islam-101-classes/ ; https://aboutislam.net/muslim-issues/n-america/embrace-helping-muslim-converts-to-belong-engage/ ; https://icna.org/embrace/
- **Confidence:** High for structure and weeks 1–9. Medium otherwise.

### MAS (Muslim American Society): Reverts Reconnect / Reverts Services (MAS New York, MAS Minnesota)
- **Organization & people:** MAS is a national nonprofit founded in 1993 with chapters. Its Tarbiya department runs a member curriculum. MAS NY has a "Reverts Reconnect" department, and MAS Minnesota runs a parallel "Reverts Reconnect."
- **Format:** Mostly live Zoom classes, with recordings on YouTube, plus weekly in-person circles at three NYC centers. Also a hotline (1-833-33MASNY), mentorship and counseling, and follow-up wellness calls.
- **Length & cadence:** Daily Qur'an reading and tafsir sessions, weekly new-Muslim classes in English and Spanish, and weekly in-person halaqas. MAS MN has monthly community circles with food and conversation. Ongoing, with no fixed end.
- **Cost:** Not stated. Free services.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims, people interested in Islam, and people "reconnecting."
- **Topics in order:** No sequenced syllabus is public. MAS NY lists aqeedah, seerah, hadith, tafsir, fiqh for daily living, and an introduction to Islamic terms. MAS MN's learning sessions (search snippet) cover (1) essential practices, (2) relationships with non-Muslim family, (3) Islamic identity at work, and (4) healthy Islamic relationships.
- **Pacing & structure:** Each revert is "offered to be placed in a halaqa" with a qualified teacher, so it's small-group and teacher-led. A "customized Shahadah experience" comes with a certificate. Converts are entered in a database and get follow-up calls and help connecting to jobs.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Holistic support covering emotional, spiritual, educational, and social needs. It's notable for including job connections and material packages.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** About **250 new-Muslim packages a year** (Qur'an translation, books, prayer mat, hijab) from MAS NY, which serves as a rough proxy for shahadas or intakes handled.
- **Strengths:** The most complete wraparound service seen: hotline, database, follow-up calls, Spanish-language classes, and jobs. It's a good model for a masjid's "convert CRM."
- **Gaps / criticisms:** No public syllabus or sequence, and no outcome data.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch of the MAS NY reverts-services and reverts-classes pages. MAS MN came from a search snippet.
- **URL(s):** https://masnewyork.org/reverts-services ; https://www.masnewyork.org/reverts-classes ; https://masnewyork.org/reverts-reconnect-1
- **Confidence:** Medium-high for MAS NY. Low for MAS MN.

### Yaqeen Institute: Convert resources, with Sh. Abdullah Oduro (covers items 4 and 6)
- **Organization & people:** Yaqeen Institute for Islamic Research (Irving, TX) is mainstream Sunni and led by Omar Suleiman. **Sh. Abdullah Oduro** is a Yaqeen scholar and a Ghanaian-American who converted in 1997. He is described as leading a Yaqeen team for "resources for new Muslim converts and institutional convert care." - **Length & cadence:** The "5 Pillars Series" is delivered as daily emails ("sign up to receive content in your inbox daily"). Episode count isn't shown. There are also the "Convert Stories" video series and "Ramadan Convert-sations" webinars, run with partners Embrace, MAS, and Ibrahim Islamic Center.

- **Intended audience:** New Muslims, Muslims rediscovering their faith, **convert coaches, and imams**. Yaqeen explicitly calls the series a tool for coaches and imams.
- **Topics in order:** 5 Pillars Series covers the who, what, when, where, and why of each pillar (1) Shahada, (2) Salah, (3) Zakat, (4) Sawm, (5) Hajj. The order is assumed to be canonical. Separate resources include "How Does Ramadan Work? A Beginner's Guide" (paper) and Convert Stories parts 1–3 (Oduro's own story, lineage, and "the path back from cultural apostasy").
- **Pacing & structure:** Daily email for the pillars series. Everything else is self-directed.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Identity- and conviction-focused ("Conviction Circle" landing page). Converts are presented as future leaders. The podcast episode "Are we failing new Muslims?" signals a retention focus.
- **Retention or outcome claims:** None.
- **Strengths:** A strong brand and credible scholar, and it already frames "convert coaches" as a role. The daily-email format is close to our daily-lesson model.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Only two items carry the "Convert Resources" tag. There's no sequenced curriculum, progress tracking, or masjid toolkit in public. I found no public page for a dedicated Oduro new-Muslim class. His personal site (abdullahoduro.com/resources) just points back to the same Yaqeen series.
- **How accessed:** WebFetch of the Yaqeen 5-pillars landing page, the convert-resources tag page, and abdullahoduro.com/resources. Search snippets for the rest.
- **URL(s):** https://pages.yaqeeninstitute.org/5-pillars ; https://yaqeeninstitute.org/tag/convert-resources ; https://yaqeeninstitute.org/series/convert-stories ; https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/are-we-failing-new-muslims-doubletake-podcast ; https://www.abdullahoduro.com/resources

### NewMuslims.com: "New Muslims eLearning Site" (and the "New Muslims" Android app)
- **Organization & people:** Run by "Distinguished Content for Communications and IT Est." with the Cooperative Office for Dawah in Rawdah (Riyadh, Saudi Arabia). It's in the same family as IslamReligion.com. Authors aren't named on the overview page. Mainstream Sunni / Saudi dawah orientation.
- **Format:** Self-paced online text lessons, each followed by a quiz. Optional registration enables progress tracking. There's an AI Q&A feature that draws on the lesson material. An Android app ("New Muslims," package com.newmuslims.sa.nmapp) says it organizes lessons "under levels." The Play Store page wouldn't render for me.
- **Length & cadence:** **10 levels and about 278 lessons** in total. Level 1 has 23 lessons, Level 2 has 25, Level 3 has 30, and so on. The pace is self-set.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Intended audience:** New converts who want to learn "in an easy and systematic way." Offered in English, Tagalog, Spanish, French, Portuguese, and Hindi.
- **Topics in order:** This is the **most complete public sequenced syllabus found.** Lesson order was pulled from the homepage (part 1/part 2 lessons combined):

    5. Paradise (2 parts)
    6. The Night Journey
    7. How to pray for a recent convert (2 parts)
    8. **Breaking the news to family** (2 parts)
    9. **Getting adjusted to the Muslim community**

    11. Belief in Allah (2 parts)
    12. Belief in prophets
    13. Belief in scriptures

  - **Level 4:** Adhan → types of shirk (3 parts) → tayammum → al-Fatiha reflections → intro to sects → protection from evil → character and purifying the soul → dress code (3 parts) → Satan → supplication → mercy of Allah → role models → **"trials and tribulations after conversion"** (2 parts).

  - **Levels 6–10:** Voluntary prayer and fasting, speech ethics, increasing faith, signs of the Hour, sexual ethics and gender interaction, intro to shariah, Eid al-Adha, bid'ah, Umrah, sins → parenting, major sins, Hajj, rightly guided caliphs, eschatology, interest/riba, short surahs, neighbors → sincerity, lawful earning, Companions, Islamic terms, khushu', dawah, good friends, **getting involved in the community**, divorce, role of scholars, sacred cities → advanced prayer, purpose of life, learning Qur'an, dhikr, morals, golden age, **social media**, **leisure and entertainment**, astrology, Meccan seerah → jihad, Prophet Adam, environment, crime and punishment, sujud al-sahw, hadith terms, Madinan seerah, creation, funerals, wills and inheritance, media stereotyping, health and fitness, intimate issues.
- **Pacing & structure:** Solo work: read a lesson, then take its quiz. No cohort and no human mentor, but there is AI Q&A.
- **Tone / philosophy:** Systematic and comprehensive, with fairly heavy creed content (shirk, omens, sects, bid'ah). Notably, Level 1 handles convert-specific social issues (telling family, adjusting to community, keeping good company) *before* the full articles of faith. It also spaces practical prayer learning across Levels 1 → 2 → 3 by repeating it at greater depth (spiral design).

- **Strengths:** A full public scope and sequence. The spiral approach to prayer is a good model. Convert-life topics are built in (family, name change, doubts, trials after conversion, social media). It's multilingual and free, with quizzes and progress tracking.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Text-heavy, with no human community or mentor and no daily rhythm. It's long (278 lessons), and topic ordering in later levels is loose. The theological framing is one school's (Saudi dawah), which may not suit every US masjid. Nothing is tailored to the US context.

## Statistics worth knowing (for the pitch)
- **Better source than "20,000/year":** ISPU's *American Mosque Survey 2020* reports average converts per mosque per year of **16.3 (2000) → 15.3 (2010) → 11.3 (2020)**, a 31% decline. It counts **2,769 US mosques** in 2020. Its implied national total is **about 31,290 converts in 2020** (33,222 in 2010). **16% of mosques reported zero conversions in 2020**, up from 2% in 2010. Converts are about 57% African American (down from 64%) and 15% Latino (up from 6%), with a roughly 60/40 male/female split. Source: [ISPU Mosque Survey 2020, Report 1](https://ispu.org/reports-and-analysis/report-1-mosque-survey-2020/) (WebFetch). Confidence: high. *This is the most defensible number for "converts per year" in the US, and it is based on mosques, which fits our buyer.*
- Pew (2018): Islam "gains about as many converts as it loses" in the US, and about 23% of US Muslim adults are converts ([Pew short read](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/01/26/the-share-of-americans-who-leave-islam-is-offset-by-those-who-become-muslim/)), via search snippet. Note: this is about people raised Muslim leaving. It does **not** measure how many converts leave.
- "Seven out of ten converts leave" comes from Imam Luqman Ahmad's 2010 blog post, an opinion piece and not a study ([imamluqman.com](https://imamluqman.com/2010/01/13/seven-out-of-every-ten-converts-leave-islam-by-imam-luqman-ahmad/)). It is widely repeated (for example by Embrace). Treat it as anecdotal.
- So the average masjid sees roughly **one new convert a month**. Per masjid that's small, but it adds up nationally, and it argues for a product that runs **asynchronously** (each convert starts on their own day) instead of waiting for a cohort to form.

## Cross-program observations
- **Typical length.** Structured courses cluster around **8–14 weekly sessions, or about 2–3.5 months.** Examples: Embrace Islam 101 (8 weeks), iERA and Embrace Step by Step (14 weeks), SeekersGuidance (14 lessons), Rabata Project Lina (9 sessions), NMA (about 1 month per course). Long tracks exist too: ICNA's 2-year program, Darul Arqam's 6-month INSTIL, and NewMuslims.com's 278 lessons. **I found no program with a true daily cadence** apart from Yaqeen's daily email drip and generic apps. A daily 60–90-day program would be unusual in this market.
- **What week 1 covers.** Almost always the **shahada and its meaning, plus an overview of the pillars and articles of faith.** Examples: iERA, Embrace, NewMuslims.com lesson 1, and Darul Arqam's BCI. **Practical prayer** (wudu and salah) shows up very early in the better programs: Ta'leef's "Establishing the Prayer" workshop, Bayyinah's course #2, and NewMuslims.com lessons 9–10. Creed-first programs (iERA, Embrace) don't reach salah until week 7–8, which is late for someone expected to pray five times a day starting now.
- **Common organizing frames.** (a) The **Hadith of Jibril triad iman / islam / ihsan** (SeekersGuidance, Darul Arqam BCI, Embrace Islam 101). (b) **Pillars of Islam as a spine** (iERA, Embrace, Yaqeen). (c) **Asad Tarsin's *Being Muslim*** as the de facto shared text across Ta'leef, SeekersGuidance, and IFN (possibly ISNA Canada's "Being Muslim" boxes too). (d) One syllabus shared between **iERA and Embrace**. So reusing curricula across organizations already happens.
- **Cohort vs. self-paced.** The market splits in two. **In-person or live programs** (Ta'leef, Embrace, MAS, iERA, masjid classes) give community but have rolling or seasonal starts and low reach. **Self-paced online** options (NewMuslims.com, NMA, SeekersGuidance, Bayyinah, apps) scale but have no human layer. **Almost nobody combines a self-paced daily curriculum with a local mentor or masjid layer.** Reverts Guide tries with mentor chat but has no traction.
- **Mentors are everywhere, but they work without content.** Mentorship is the most common element: Ta'leef consultations, WhyIslam, the Mecca Center (mentors have 5+ years as converts), iERA mentoring, IFN's named mentor team, MAS halaqa placement, and Rabata's one-to-one care. But mentor programs **rarely come with a curriculum or progress view** (the Mecca Center's has "no curriculum," and IFN refers out to 3–5 providers). A tool that gives a mentor "where my mentee is today and what to discuss" fills a visible gap.
- **What's commonly missing.**
  1. Telling family and dealing with non-Muslim relatives (covered only by NewMuslims.com L1, Rabata's "Tend Your Ties," and MAS MN)
  2. Identity and culture vs. religion, plus name changes (Rabata, NewMuslims.com L5)
  3. Doubts and spiritual dips (NewMuslims.com L4–5, iERA's last 3 sessions)
  4. Navigating the masjid and its social norms (NewMuslims.com L1/L5, Darul Arqam's mosque visit)
  5. Ramadan and Eid as "first-time" events (Yaqeen, Embrace, and MAS do seasonal one-offs)
  6. Work, money, and daily logistics (NMA "Muslims at Work/Wealth," MAS job connections)
  7. Any measured outcomes
- **Who publishes results.** Very few, and only activity counts:
  - Ta'leef: "over 500" helped embrace Islam through Welcome to the Fold
  - Embrace: 300–350 active participants
  - MAS NY: about 250 packages a year
  - Markfield: about 100 participants a year
  - IslamInSpanish: "100s" of shahadas
  - **No program publishes completion or retention rates.** A product that reports retention at 30/90/365 days would be first-of-kind evidence and a selling point to masjid boards.
- **Evidence masjids would pay.** It's weak and indirect, so treat this as a hypothesis to test.
  - Almost all convert programs are **free and volunteer-run**. The only paid items found are IslamInSpanish seminars ($20–50, paid by learners), Bayyinah TV ($11/mo, paid by learners), and Rabata's workshop fee.
  - On the other hand:
    - Embrace sells or offers **"Convert Sensitivity Training"** to communities.
    - Yaqeen explicitly makes content for **"convert coaches and Imams."**
    - ISNA Canada, MAS NY, and others **already spend money on welcome boxes and packages.** MAS NY sends about 250 a year, which signals per-convert budget.
    - The masjid-software market (Masjidbox, ConnectMazjid, Masjidkit, Ummah) mostly uses **free-core or donation-share pricing**, not flat SaaS fees ([theummah.io comparison](https://theummah.io/blog/free-mosque-management-software-comparison/)). That's a pricing-pressure signal.
  - I **found no evidence of a masjid paying for a convert-care curriculum or tool.** The most plausible paths:
    - (a) fold it into the welcome-box or package budget
    - (b) get it sponsored by a donor or national org (ICNA, MAS, Yaqeen) and give it free to masjids
    - (c) a low per-convert fee with a free tier, mirroring masjid-software norms
- **Positioning implication.** No US program combines all four of: (1) a **public, sequenced** scope and sequence, (2) a **daily** cadence, (3) a **masjid or mentor dashboard**, and (4) **published outcomes**. Darul Arqam (Singapore) is the closest institutional model for structure. NewMuslims.com is the closest for content breadth. Ta'leef and Embrace are closest for belonging and community.
