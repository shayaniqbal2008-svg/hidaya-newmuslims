# Research Notes: Core Essentials and Madhab Map

> [!warning] Draft for scholar review. Not for direct use in lessons.
> This file maps what mainstream Sunni sources teach. It does not issue rulings. Where sources disagree, both positions are recorded without resolving them. Every claim should be checked by a qualified scholar before it reaches a lesson.


## How to read this file

- **Confidence:** high = 2+ independent Tier-1 sources agree and the point is widely stated; medium = 2 sources but secondary or summary-level, or minor nuance unresolved; low = thin sourcing.
- **single-source** = only one reliable source found. Needs a scholar to confirm.
- **Leaning tags:** [Hanafi], [Shafi'i], [Maliki], [Hanbali], [Salafi-leaning], [multi-madhab], [academic]. See the Source list at the end.
- Hadith references cite sunnah.com numbering. sunnah.com blocked automated fetching during this session (HTTP 403), so those numbers came from secondary Tier-1 sources that cite them. A human should click through and check them before use.
- Some web-search summary tools misdescribed madhab positions (for example, saying the Hanafis recite Fatiha behind the imam in silent prayers). Every madhab cell below was checked against a page from that school's own scholars, or against a multi-madhab fatwa, wherever possible.

## The essentials: what every new Muslim needs first

### 1. Shahada

**(a) What it is.** The shahada is the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His Messenger. Saying it sincerely, with understanding and acceptance, is what makes a person Muslim. It is the first of the five pillars.
**(b) Why early.** It is the entry point, and everything else builds on it. In the hadith of Mu'adh, the shahada is the first thing people are called to, before prayer or zakat ([Bukhari 1395](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1395); see #9).
**(c) Minimum now.**
- **Conditions:** sincere conviction, understanding the meaning, and accepting Islam with its obligations. Source: [SeekersGuidance: Does taking shahada require witnesses?](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/calling-to-islam/does-taking-shahada-require-witnesses/); [IslamWeb 11140](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/11140/ruling-on-ghusl-for-those-embracing-islam) (the two testimonies with conviction are what is required). Confidence: high.
- **Witnesses are not required for validity.** Declaring it alone is enough. Historically, witnesses served legal and administrative purposes (marriage, inheritance, Hajj paperwork), and a masjid certificate is recommended but optional. Sources: [SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdullah Anik Misra, rev. Shaykh Faraz Rabbani)](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/calling-to-islam/does-taking-shahada-require-witnesses/) [Hanafi/traditionalist]; [IslamQA.org/Muftionline 98069](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftionline/98069/converting-to-islam-in-a-non-islamic-country-witnessing-the-shahada-and-burial-rights/) [Hanafi]. Confidence: high.
- **Do not delay the shahada** for a ghusl, a ceremony or more witnesses. Al-Nawawi called delaying it for ghusl seriously prohibited. Sources: [AMJA paper, Dr. Jamaal Zarabozo (2017)](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf) (quotes al-Nawawi, al-Majmu' 2:154); the same paper cites the Saudi Standing Committee (via [IslamQA.info 1163](https://islamqa.info/en/1163)) that circumcision should not be insisted on if it would turn someone away. Confidence: high for "do not delay" (classical, quoted). Circumcision is single-source here and out of week-one scope.
- **Ghusl on conversion. This is a genuine difference of opinion.**
  - *Obligatory for every convert:* the well-known Hanbali view, one view attributed to Malik, and Ibn Taymiyyah, Abu Thawr and Ibn al-Mundhir. The evidence is that the Prophet ﷺ ordered Qays ibn 'Asim to bathe when he accepted Islam (Abu Dawud 355; Tirmidhi 605; Nasa'i 188). Sources: [AMJA/Zarabozo](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf); [MuslimMatters, Abdullah Hasan (quotes Ibn Qudamah)](https://muslimmatters.org/2017/02/13/is-ghusl-obligatory-upon-the-new-muslim-abdullah-hasan/).
  - *Recommended, and obligatory only if the person is in a state of major impurity (janaba, or after menstruation or postnatal bleeding):* the relied-upon Hanafi and Shafi'i view. Sources: [Darul Ifta Birmingham via IslamQA.org 19848](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa-birmingham/19848/is-a-convert-required-to-have-a-ghusl-when-accepting-islam/) (cites Nur al-Idah) [Hanafi]; [IslamWeb 11140](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/11140/ruling-on-ghusl-for-those-embracing-islam) (reports it as the predominant view; Hanafi, Shafi'i, and some Hanbalis such as al-Mardawi).
  - *Zarabozo (AMJA paper, author's own view, not AMJA's):* there is no strong evidence of a ghusl specifically *for* conversion. But since nearly every adult convert has had prior janaba or menstruation, ghusl is in practice needed before the first prayer.
  - **All sources agree that ghusl is not a condition for the conversion itself to be valid.** Confidence: high on the existence and shape of the disagreement.
- **A new name is not required.** A name must be changed only if its meaning conflicts with Islamic belief (for example, one that means "servant of" someone other than Allah) or is clearly bad. Otherwise changing it is optional. Family names (surnames) should be kept, out of respect for lineage (Qur'an 33:5). Sources: [IslamOnline, Dr. Muzammil Siddiqi (then Fiqh Council of North America president)](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/is-changing-name-necessary-for-new-convert/); [IslamWeb 393040](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/393040/changing-his-name-after-embracing-islam); [AMJA/Zarabozo](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf) (quotes Ibn Baz: no evidence requires a new name; changing it is obligatory only for names implying worship of other than Allah). Confidence: **high (3 sources, across leanings)**.
**(e) Differences:** ghusl (above). Ibn Baz is quoted as saying it is "good" to change a name widely recognized as Christian, which is softer than an obligation. Whether to *encourage* a name change is a pastoral choice. → Scholar review.

### 2. The six articles of faith and the five pillars (Hadith of Jibril)

**(a) What it is.** In the Hadith of Jibril, the angel Jibril came in human form and asked the Prophet ﷺ about Islam, Iman and Ihsan. **Islam** = the five pillars: the shahada, the five daily prayers, zakat, fasting Ramadan, and Hajj for those able. **Iman** = belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree (qadar), both its good and its bad. **Ihsan** = to worship Allah as though you see Him, knowing that He sees you even though you do not see Him.
**(b) Why early.** Scholars treat this hadith as a summary of the whole religion. The Prophet ﷺ ended it by saying Jibril had come to teach people their religion. It gives a ready-made three-part framework for a curriculum.
**(c) Minimum now.** Believe in the six articles at once (they are part of what the shahada commits to). Detailed creed ('aqidah) can be learned over time.
**(d) Sources.** Sahih Muslim 8 ([sunnah.com/muslim:8a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:8a)); Bukhari 50 ([sunnah.com/bukhari:50](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:50)); al-Nawawi's Forty Hadith #2 ([sunnah.com/nawawi40:2](https://sunnah.com/nawawi40:2)). *These URLs returned 403 to automated fetching. The citations are standard and were confirmed via the secondary sources below.* [Yaqeen Institute: Basics of Islam](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/what-islam-says-about/basics-of-islam) (six articles, five pillars); [SeekersGuidance: Ibn Rajab's commentary on the Hadith of Jibril](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/ibn-rajab-al-hanbalis-commentary-on-the-hadith-of-gibril/). Confidence: **high**.
**(e) Differences.** None of substance on the framing within Sunni Islam.

### 3. Tahara basics: wudu, ghusl, tayammum, istinja

**(a) What it is.**
- **Wudu** (ablution) is required before prayer. Qur'an 5:6 names four acts all schools agree are obligatory: wash the face, wash the arms to and including the elbows, wipe the head, and wash the feet to and including the ankles. The schools add further requirements. Intention is required by the Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools (sunnah for Hanafis). Sequence is required by the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools. Continuity is required by the Maliki and Hanbali schools. Rubbing is required by the Maliki school. Sources: [quran.com/5/6](https://quran.com/5/6); [SeekersGuidance (Hanafi): forgetting parts of wudu and continuity](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/forgetting-parts-of-ritual-ablution-wudu-and-maintaining-continuity/); [My Religion Islam: obligatory acts of wudu (comparative)](https://myreligionislam.com/detail.asp?Aid=6073). Confidence: high on the four agreed acts; **medium** on the per-school additions (the comparative source is not Tier-1, so a scholar should confirm).
- **What breaks wudu. Agreed:** anything leaving the front or back passage (urine, feces, wind, pre-seminal fluid), and deep sleep or loss of consciousness. **Disputed:** touching the opposite sex, bleeding, and touching one's private parts (see the Madhab map). Sources: [IslamQA.info 76115](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/76115); [IslamQA.info 88040](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/88040); [SeekersGuidance: Hanafi list of nullifiers](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/could-you-please-list-all-the-nullifiers-of-ablution-according-to-the-hanafi-school/). Confidence: high for the agreed list.
- **Ghusl** (full bath) is required after sexual intercourse (even without ejaculation), after ejaculation (including in sleep), when menstruation ends, and when postnatal bleeding ends. One ghusl covers both janaba and menstruation. Sources: [IslamQA.info 243792](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/243792); [IslamQA.info 128877](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/128877); [IslamQA.org/SeekersGuidance Shafi'i 169593](https://islamqa.org/shafii/seekersguidance-shafii/169593/my-menstruation-started-while-i-was-in-janaba/). Confidence: high.
- **Tayammum** (dry ablution with clean earth) replaces wudu or ghusl when there is no water or when using water would cause harm (for example, illness). It involves wiping the face and the hands or arms. Schools differ on one strike or two, and on wrists versus elbows. Sources: Qur'an 4:43 and 5:6 ([quran.com/4/43](https://quran.com/4/43)); [IslamQA.info 13618](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/13618); [IslamQA.info 40204](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/40204). Confidence: high that it is allowed; **low on the method details** (only non-Tier-1 comparative sources were found). → Scholar to supply the method to teach.
- **Istinja** means cleaning the private parts after using the toilet, with water or with paper/stones. Removing filth (najasa) from body, clothes and place is a condition of prayer. *Single-source in this session (general knowledge, not separately verified). Scholar should confirm the teaching text.* Confidence: medium.
**(b) Why early.** Prayer is not valid without purity, and prayer is due from the first day (see #4).
**(c) Minimum now / what is excused.** SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdul-Rahim Reasat) describes the minimum wudu as washing the face, the arms to the elbows and the feet, and wiping (part of) the head. This can be learned in minutes. ([IslamQA.org/SeekersGuidance 167692](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/seekersguidance-hanafi/167692/is-it-permissible-to-just-read-the-quran-until-i-learn-how-to-pray/)). Honest mistakes made before learning are not sinful ([SeekersGuidance: How can a convert follow a school correctly](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/how-can-a-convert-follow-a-school-of-thought-correctly/)). A woman who converts during her period does her ghusl when it ends ([AMJA/Zarabozo](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf)). IslamQA.info 193008 addresses whether a new Muslim must repeat prayers done wrongly out of ignorance ([link](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/193008)). *Not fetched, title only, so verify.* Confidence: medium.
**(e) Differences:** see the Madhab map rows (touching, bleeding, socks).

### 4. Salah

**(a) What it is.** Five obligatory daily prayers: **Fajr** (dawn, 2 rak'ahs), **Dhuhr** (after midday, 4), **Asr** (afternoon, 4), **Maghrib** (after sunset, 3) and **Isha** (night, 4). Each has a time window. The prayer is made facing the qibla (the Ka'bah in Makkah; Qur'an 2:144). From most of the US, the qibla is roughly northeast. Sources: [Yaqeen: Basics of Islam](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/what-islam-says-about/basics-of-islam); [IslamQA.org/SeekersGuidance 167692](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/seekersguidance-hanafi/167692/is-it-permissible-to-just-read-the-quran-until-i-learn-how-to-pray/) (rak'ah counts); [quran.com/2/144](https://quran.com/2/144). Confidence: high.
**(b) Why early.** It is the first practical obligation after the shahada in the Mu'adh hadith ([Bukhari 1395](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1395)), and the pillar most emphasized after belief.
**(c) Minimum now. Important nuance for a staged curriculum:**
- **All five prayers are obligatory from the first day, and learning is not a reason to delay them.** SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdul-Rahim Reasat, rev. Shaykh Faraz Rabbani): a convert should start praying all five at once, doing the bare minimum of actions while learning. [IslamQA.org/SeekersGuidance 167692](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/seekersguidance-hanafi/167692/is-it-permissible-to-just-read-the-quran-until-i-learn-how-to-pray/). IslamWeb: none of the five may be dropped, and advising a new Muslim to "start with two" risks making them think there is a real concession for this. [IslamWeb 529861](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/529861/the-obligation-of-the-five-daily-prayers-for-a-new-muslim). Confidence: **high (2 Tier-1 sources)**. *Note: some popular app and blog guides suggest "start with one prayer and add more". The Tier-1 sources above explicitly disagree. The curriculum should not present a reduced number of prayers as allowed.*
- **Prayer is due from the time of conversion onward. Prayers missed before Islam are not made up.** Sources: [IslamWeb 243298](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/243298/does-the-person-who-embraces-islam-have-to-perform-the-prayers-he-missed-on-that-day) (pray the prayer currently due; scholars differ on whether the earlier prayer of a combinable pair, e.g. Dhuhr when converting in Asr time, is also owed); [IslamQA.org/Qibla Shafi'i 34014](https://islamqa.org/shafii/qibla-shafii/34014/make-up-prayers-of-a-new-convert/). Confidence: high on "no make-up of pre-Islam prayers"; the combinable-pair question is contested.
- **What to say before knowing Arabic** (see #5).
- **Reading from a card or phone during prayer.** Hanafi: SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Faraz Rabbani) says *holding* the Qur'an in prayer is prohibitively disliked, and in the relied-upon Hanafi view reading from a mushaf can invalidate the prayer. The convert should instead memorize a short verse such as al-Ikhlas ([IslamQA.org/Qibla 35467](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/35467/how-do-new-muslims-recite-in-prayer/)). Shafi'i and Hanbali: reading from a mushaf in prayer is allowed (per [IslamQA.info 108242](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/108242), reported but not fetched). **This directly affects a "pray with a card" feature. → Scholar review.** Confidence: medium.
- **Obligatory vs. sunnah components.** All schools distinguish the pillars (arkan: e.g. opening takbir, standing, recitation, ruku', sujud, final sitting and salam/exit) from sunnah acts (e.g. raising the hands, opening supplication). The Hanafis add a middle category (wajib). SeekersGuidance notes that in Hanafi fiqh a wajib act may be left for a valid excuse, and being a new Muslim is such an excuse ([Qibla 35467](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/35467/how-do-new-muslims-recite-in-prayer/)). *The exact list of arkan differs by school. Not separately mapped. → Scholar review.* Confidence: medium.
- **Jumu'ah** (Friday congregational prayer, replacing Dhuhr) is obligatory on adult, sane, resident men without an excuse. Women, travelers and the sick are exempt, and if they attend it counts. Sources: [IslamQA.info 2905](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/2905); [IslamWeb 82835: conditions of Jumu'ah](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/82835/conditions-of-the-jumu%E2%80%99ah-prayer). Confidence: high.
- **Congregational prayer (for men, the five daily prayers).** Hanafi: sunnah mu'akkadah (some Hanafis say wajib). Maliki: sunnah mu'akkadah. Shafi'i: communal obligation (fard kifayah) in the relied-upon view. Hanbali: individually obligatory (wajib) for men. All agree a prayer made alone is valid. Sources: [IslamQA.org Shafi'i Fiqh 30069](https://islamqa.org/shafii/shafiifiqh/30069/is-congregational-prayer-obligatory/); [SeekersGuidance: ruling on congregation](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/guidance-answers/what-is-the-ruling-on-performing-prayer-in-congregation-salat-al-jamaa/). Confidence: medium (from summaries; not fully fetched).
**(e) Differences.** The prayer method itself differs by school (see the Madhab map). Asr and Isha times differ. Congregation status differs.

### 5. Learning al-Fatiha and short surahs: the transition period

**(a) What it is.** Al-Fatiha (the first surah) is recited in every rak'ah. The majority hold it is a pillar of the prayer. Hanafis hold it is wajib, while some Qur'an recitation is the fard.
**(b) Why early.** It is needed for every prayer from day one.
**(c) Minimum now: what scholars say to do before memorizing.**
- **Majority view (Shafi'i, Hanbali, Maliki, and contemporary bodies):** until Fatiha is learned, say simple words of remembrance (dhikr) in its place: *subhan Allah, al-hamdu lillah, la ilaha illa Allah, Allahu akbar, la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah*. This is based on the hadith of Ibn Abi Awfa (Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i; per Zarabozo, graded hasan by al-Albani. The number is commonly given as Abu Dawud 832, so verify), where the Prophet ﷺ taught these words to a man who could not learn any Qur'an, and on the Rifa'ah ibn Rafi' hadith (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi). Learning Fatiha as soon as reasonably possible is itself an obligation. Sources: [IslamQA.info 5410](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/5410) [Salafi-leaning]; [Dar al-Ifta Egypt 8030](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/8030/i-am-a-new-muslim-and-unable-to-recite-fatiha-chapter-during-prayers-what-should) [Azhari/Shafi'i] (cites Ibn Qudamah; says the struggling learner gets a double reward); [AMJA/Zarabozo](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf). Confidence: **high**.
- **Hanafi view (SeekersGuidance, Shaykh Faraz Rabbani):** a new Muslim is legally excused from the wajib recitation while learning. They should ideally recite a short verse they can manage (e.g. "Qul huwa Allahu ahad") and take reasonable steps to learn. [IslamQA.org/Qibla 35467](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/35467/how-do-new-muslims-recite-in-prayer/). Confidence: medium (one Hanafi source fetched; a second Hanafi source is listed at [Mathabah](https://www.mathabah.org/how-does-a-new-muslim-recite-during-ritual-prayer/) but was not fetched).
- **Reciting a *translation* of Fatiha. Contested.** The majority hold that the Qur'an must be recited in Arabic and that a translation is not Qur'an and does not count. The early Hanafi report attributes to Abu Hanifa permission to recite in Persian. Later Hanafi authorities (e.g. Abu Bakr al-Razi, as cited by al-Laknawi) report that he retracted, limiting it to one *unable* to recite Arabic. Sources: [AMJA/Zarabozo](https://www.amjaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/A-Note-on-Ibaadah-Related-Practices-for-a-Muslim-Convert-AMJA-Annual-Conference-2017.pdf) (detailed); [IslamQA.info 291352: Quran in English during prayer](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/291352) (title only, not fetched). Dhikr phrases (as opposed to Qur'an) are more widely allowed in translation (Zarabozo). → **Scholar review: should the app ever show a translation to recite?**
**(e) Differences:** above.

### 6. Halal and haram basics for week one

**(a) What it is.**
- **Food.** The Qur'an prohibits pork, carrion, blood, and what is dedicated to other than Allah (Qur'an 2:173, 5:3, 6:145). It prohibits intoxicants (khamr) (Qur'an 5:90–91). Food of the People of the Book is permitted in principle (Qur'an 5:5), but **whether ordinary US commercial meat qualifies is contested** (see the Madhab map row). Sources: [quran.com/2/173](https://quran.com/2/173); [quran.com/5/3](https://quran.com/5/3); [quran.com/5/90](https://quran.com/5/90); [quran.com/5/5](https://quran.com/5/5); [AMJA 83915](https://www.amjaonline.org/fatwa/en/83915/difference-between-zabihah-and-non-zabihah); [HFSAA](https://www.hfsaa.org/articles/ahlul-kitab-meat). Confidence: high on pork and alcohol; **contested** on US meat.
- **Modesty ('awrah).** For men, the 'awrah is navel to knee (Hanafis include the knee). For women, in the dominant view, it is the whole body except the face and hands (views differ on the feet). Covering the 'awrah is a condition of prayer. Sources: [IslamQA.org Hanbali Disciples: female 'awrah in prayer](https://islamqa.org/hanbali/hanbalidisciples/153952/female-awrah-in-prayer/); [IslamQA.org/Qibla 42657](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/42657/details-on-uncovered-nakedness-awra-and-the-validity-of-prayer-5/). Confidence: medium (summary-level).
- **Hijab** is held to be obligatory by mainstream scholars (Qur'an 24:31, 33:59). **How to approach it with a convert is handled gradually by several scholars** (see #9). Sources: [IslamQA.org/Qibla, Shaykh Gibril Haddad](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/35720/converts-hijab-the-question-of-gradualism/); [IslamOnline, Shaykh Yusuf al-Qaradawi](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/new-muslims-should-hijab-be-an-obstacle/). Confidence: high that it is obligatory per these sources and that they advise a gradual approach.
- **Interactions: handshakes with the opposite sex. Contested.** Majority: not permitted (except, for some Hanafis and Hanbalis, with the elderly where there is no desire). Evidence includes the Prophet ﷺ saying he did not shake hands with women (al-Nasa'i). Mathabah (Shaykh Yusuf Badat, Hanafi) suggests a hand-on-heart greeting at work. **Dar al-Ifta Egypt** permits following the view that allows a handshake without desire, especially in non-Muslim countries. Sources: [Mathabah via IslamQA.org 133926](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/mathabah/133926/gender-interaction-in-islam-shaking-hands/); [Dar al-Ifta Egypt 6808](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6808/can-i-shake-hands-with-non-mahram-women); [IslamQA.info 21183](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/21183) (title only). Confidence: high that it is contested.
**(b) Why early.** These come up at the first meal, the first workday and the first family visit.
**(c) Urgent vs. gradual.** The prohibitions of pork and alcohol are not suspended for converts. However, scholars distinguish *denying* a prohibition (a creedal problem) from *struggling* to leave it (a sin, with repentance always open). A person struggling with alcohol must keep praying. Sources: [IslamWeb 281668](https://islamweb.net/amp/en/fatwa/281668) (persistent drinker must not stop praying); [IslamWeb 10399](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/10399/ruling-on-someone-who-drinks-alcohol-and-observes-prayer-and-fasting). Confidence: high (2 sources, same institution, so treat as medium-high). Gradual *teaching* order is supported by the Aisha narration (#9).
**(e) Differences:** US meat, handshakes, the extent of women's 'awrah (feet), and how gradual hijab adoption can be. → Scholar review.

### 7. Zakat, fasting and Hajj: now vs. later

**Zakat.** (a) An annual payment (usually 2.5% of qualifying savings) due when wealth at or above the nisab threshold has been held for one lunar year (hawl). (b) It is the third item in the Mu'adh hadith. (c) **Now:** nothing is due immediately on conversion, and nothing is owed for pre-Islam years. The first hawl starts when the person, as a Muslim, holds nisab-level wealth. Sources: [IslamQA.org/Qibla Shafi'i 34014](https://islamqa.org/shafii/qibla-shafii/34014/make-up-prayers-of-a-new-convert/) (no make-up of pre-Islam obligations; read for prayer, extended by analogy); [MuslimMatters, Abdullah Hasan](https://muslimmatters.org/2017/02/13/is-ghusl-obligatory-upon-the-new-muslim-abdullah-hasan/) (cites Ibn al-Humam: a non-Muslim is not addressed by the subsidiary rulings before Islam; Qur'an 8:38; the hadith "Islam erases what came before it", which is Sahih Muslim 121 per standard numbering, so verify on sunnah.com). **"Hawl starts at conversion" is single-source / inferred. No Tier-1 page stating it explicitly for converts was found. Scholar to confirm.** Confidence: medium.

**Fasting (Ramadan).** (a) Fasting from dawn to sunset through Ramadan is obligatory for adult, sane, healthy, resident Muslims. Exemptions with make-up apply for illness, travel, menstruation and postnatal bleeding. ([Yaqeen: How does Ramadan work? A beginner's guide](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/how-does-ramadan-work-a-beginners-guide)). (c) **A convert mid-Ramadan:**
- Does **not** make up the Ramadan days before conversion. This is agreed by both sources below.
- **The rest of the conversion day. Contested.** IslamQA.info: they **must** refrain from eating for the rest of that day, but do **not** make it up ([IslamQA.info 112150](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/112150/does-he-have-to-refrain-from-eating-on-the-day-he-becomes-muslim)) [Salafi-leaning, follows Ibn 'Uthaymin]. Shafi'i school (SeekersGuidance, Shaykh Jamir Meah): refraining is **recommended**, not obligatory, and making up that day is **sunnah** ([SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/fiqh-fasting-ramadan-according-school-imam-shafii/)). Confidence: high that it is contested.

**Hajj.** Pilgrimage to Makkah once in a lifetime for those physically and financially able. It is not a week-one item. A convert learns it when it becomes relevant (the IslamQA.info 109201 teaching order: tawhid → prayer → zakat → fasting → Hajj "when its time comes"). [IslamQA.info 109201](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/109201); [Yaqeen: Basics](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/what-islam-says-about/basics-of-islam). Confidence: high.

### 8. Non-Muslim family

**(a) What the tradition says.** Kindness and good companionship toward parents is required even when they are not Muslim, and even if they push the child toward shirk. The child does not obey them in that, but still treats them well in worldly matters (Qur'an 31:14–15, 29:8). Kindness and justice toward non-Muslims who are not hostile is permitted and praised (Qur'an 60:8). Asma' bint Abi Bakr asked whether to keep ties with her mother, who was not Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ told her to keep ties with her (Bukhari 2620; Muslim 1003, standard numbering, not fetched; the narration is also summarized in IslamQA.info 322). Sources: [quran.com/31/14-15](https://quran.com/31/14-15); [quran.com/60/8](https://quran.com/60/8); [IslamQA.info 322: treatment of a non-Muslim mother](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/322); [SeekersGuidance: Attending Christmas festivities of one's non-Muslim family](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/attending-christmas-festivities-of-ones-non-muslim-family/) (keeping family ties). Confidence: **high** on kindness to parents.
**(b) Why early.** Family reaction is often the first and hardest test. Scholars and convert-care sources warn against cutting off family (see #10).
**(c) Holidays and family events. Contested, scholar-review item.**
- *Attend the social gathering, and avoid the religious rites:* SeekersGuidance (Hanafi; Sidi Waseem Hussain, rev. Shaykh Faraz Rabbani; cites Ibn 'Abidin) says a convert may attend family Christmas gatherings as keeping family ties, while quietly not taking part in religious activities ([link](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/attending-christmas-festivities-of-ones-non-muslim-family/)). Dr. Jamal Badawi and Shaykh Ahmad Kutty say the same, avoiding alcohol, pork and religious rites ([IslamOnline](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/attending-christmas-with-non-muslim-relatives/)).
- *Greeting ("Merry Christmas"):* **Dar al-Ifta Egypt** says it is permissible with words that do not contradict Islamic belief ([6815](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6815/39-congratulating-christians-on-the-occasion-of-christmas)). **IslamQA.info** says it is prohibited, citing a consensus reported by Ibn al-Qayyim ([947](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/947/ruling-on-celebrating-non-muslim-holidays-and-congratulating-them)).
- *Participation:* **IslamQA.info** says it is not permissible to take part in the festivals "in any way", including parties and gift exchanges, and advises excusing oneself ([145986](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/145986)). **AMJA** (2006 fatwa, Dr. Main al-Qudah) says celebrating Thanksgiving, Mother's Day and Father's Day is not lawful, because they are tied to other religions ([22994](https://www.amjaonline.org/fatwa/en/22994/celebrating-non-muslim-holidays)). *That fatwa does not address a convert attending a family meal.*
- Confidence: high that the range of views is accurately recorded. **Do not resolve.**

### 9. Gradualism (tadarruj)

**(a) What it is.** The principle that religious teaching is given in stages: the most important first, then the next. This rests on how the Qur'an itself was revealed.
- **Hadith of Mu'adh:** when sending Mu'adh ibn Jabal to Yemen, the Prophet ﷺ told him to call people first to the shahada, then (if they accepted) to teach them the five prayers, then zakat. [Bukhari 1395](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1395); also Muslim 19. Confirmed by [IslamQA.info 109201](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/109201) and [IslamWeb 11140](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/11140/ruling-on-ghusl-for-those-embracing-islam). Confidence: high.
- **Aisha's narration:** the first parts of the Qur'an revealed spoke of Paradise and Hell. Only once people's hearts had settled on Islam were rulings on halal and haram revealed. Had "do not drink wine" come first, people would have refused. [Bukhari 4993](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4993). Confidence: high (the citation is standard; the search snippet matched it).
- **Scholarly statements applied to new Muslims:** IslamQA.info 109201 says a new Muslim should be taught in stages, not all at once. Start with belief and the obligations, let Islam settle in the heart, then proceed from the most important to the next (Qur'an 16:125). SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdullah Anik Misra, a convert): "a marathon, not a sprint"; make changes gradually ([link](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/seeking-knowledge/how-should-a-recent-convert-build-their-knowledge-of-islam/)). Confidence: **high (2 sources across leanings)**.
**(b) Why it matters.** It justifies a staged curriculum.
**(c) The key limit, where sources agree:** gradualism applies to **teaching order and pastoral patience**. **Known obligations are not cancelled.** IslamQA.info 109201 stages *instruction*, not the obligation itself. IslamWeb 529861 and SeekersGuidance 167692 say all five prayers apply from day one. Confidence: high.
**(e) Where sources differ: how far tolerance stretches.** Shaykh Gibril Haddad says hijab "stands as prescribed", but a convert may be helped toward it step by step ([Qibla 35720](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/qibla-hanafi/35720/converts-hijab-the-question-of-gradualism/)). Shaykh Yusuf al-Qaradawi goes further: one may accept, for a time, a convert's uncovered head, though it is unlawful, for fear she leaves Islam, weighing harms and benefits ([IslamOnline](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/new-muslims-should-hijab-be-an-obstacle/)). IslamWeb 529861 warns that presenting a reduced practice as allowed may mislead the convert into thinking there is a real concession. **→ Scholar review: the wording the app uses for "you are still learning X".**

## Madhab map (light touch)

### The four schools in one paragraph each

**Hanafi.** Named for Imam Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man ibn Thabit (d. 150 AH / 767 CE) of Kufa, Iraq. It was developed by his students Abu Yusuf and Muhammad al-Shaybani, and later Hanafi doctrine sometimes follows a student over the founder. It is known for its developed method of juristic reasoning (ra'y, qiyas, istihsan). Today it is the largest school, dominant in Turkey, the Balkans, Central Asia and South Asia (Pakistan, India, Bangladesh). In the US it is common in South Asian and Turkish masjids. Sources: [Wikipedia: Madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab) [academic overview]; [IslamWeb: period of the four imams](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86509/the-period-in-which-the-four-imams-lived) [multi-madhab, Gulf]; [Yaqeen: What is a Madhhab?](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/what-is-a-madhhab-exploring-the-role-of-islamic-schools-of-law) (on later Hanafi scholars differing from the founder). Confidence: high.

**Maliki.** Named for Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH / 795–796 CE) of Madinah, author of al-Muwatta'. A distinctive feature is that it treats the living practice ('amal) of the people of Madinah as a source of law. It is dominant in North and West Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania, Senegal, Mali, Nigeria and others), in Upper Egypt, and in parts of the Gulf. In the US it is common among West and North African communities and in some convert-led and Zaytuna-influenced circles. Sources: [Wikipedia: Madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab); [IslamWeb 86509](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86509/the-period-in-which-the-four-imams-lived); [Virtual Mosque: Why do the Malikis pray with hands at their sides](https://www.virtualmosque.com/islam-studies/why-do-the-malikis-pray-with-their-hands-to-the-sides-part-one/) (on 'amal of Madinah) [Maliki]. Confidence: high.

**Shafi'i.** Named for Imam Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (150–204 AH / 767–820 CE). He studied under Malik and with Hanafi scholars, and died in Egypt. He is credited with systematizing legal theory (usul al-fiqh) in al-Risala. The school is dominant in Egypt (Lower), Yemen, the Levant (alongside Hanafi), East Africa (Somalia, the Swahili coast), Kurdistan and Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei). In the US it is common among Somali, Yemeni, Southeast Asian and many Arab communities. Sources: [Wikipedia: Madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab); [Wikipedia: al-Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shafi'i); [IslamWeb 86509](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86509/the-period-in-which-the-four-imams-lived). Confidence: high.

**Hanbali.** Named for Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (164–241 AH / 780–855 CE) of Baghdad, a student of al-Shafi'i, a great hadith scholar and compiler of the Musnad. The school is known for staying close to hadith and the reports of the Companions, and for using analogy sparingly. It is the smallest of the four by followers and predominant in Saudi Arabia and Qatar. Many Gulf-trained and Salafi-leaning scholars draw on Hanbali texts, while some describe themselves as not bound to any one madhab. Sources: [Wikipedia: Madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab); [IslamWeb 86509](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86509/the-period-in-which-the-four-imams-lived). Confidence: high on founder and dates, medium on "general character" (characterizations vary by who is writing).

> All four schools regard each other as valid Sunni schools. Differences in the table below do not invalidate the prayer of someone following another school. Sources: [SeekersGuidance on raising the hands (Hanafi)](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/prayer/what-is-the-ruling-and-method-of-raising-the-hands-for-the-opening-takbir/) ("a respected difference … not an innovation"); [AMJA on wiping socks](https://www.amjaonline.org/fatwa/en/78081/wiping-on-the-socks) (such differences should not stop Muslims praying behind one imam); [SeekersGuidance, Shafi'i, basmala quietly to avoid discord](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/can-recite-basmalah-quietly-leading-prayer-avoid-discord-shafii/). Confidence: high.

## How respected scholars advise converts to handle madhab choice

Views fall along a range. They are recorded here without resolving them.

**1. Following one of the four schools is obligatory for a non-scholar (traditionalist view).**
- A fatwa by Shaykh Murabtal Haaj (Mauritania, Maliki), translated by **Hamza Yusuf** (Zaytuna College), holds that anyone who is not an absolute mujtahid must follow qualified scholarship. It says the consensus has settled on the four schools and warns against piecemeal picking. [masud.co.uk](https://masud.co.uk/shaykh-murabtal-haajs-fatwa-on-following-one-of-the-four-accepted-madhhabs/) [Maliki / traditionalist]
- Darul Ifta Birmingham holds that a layperson must adhere to one of the four schools, so as not to choose rulings by personal convenience. It uses the example of bleeding and wudu. [IslamQA.org/Darul Ifta Birmingham 135125](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa-birmingham/135125/is-it-necessary-to-follow-any-one-of-the-four-imams/) [Hanafi]
- **Yaqeen Institute** (Dr. Emad Hamdeh) argues that laypeople should follow a madhhab. Following qualified scholars is the layperson's way of following scripture, and it gives legal stability. [Yaqeen](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/what-is-a-madhhab-exploring-the-role-of-islamic-schools-of-law) [academic, pro-madhhab]

**2. Strongly recommended, and chosen on practical grounds (the advice SeekersGuidance gives converts).**
- SeekersGuidance (reviewed by Shaykh Faraz Rabbani) advises a convert to choose a school they can **learn properly**, can **get questions answered in**, and that fits their **family, community and inclination**. [SeekersGuidance: As a convert how do I choose](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/as-a-convert-how-do-i-choose-a-school-of-thought/) [Hanafi/Shafi'i traditionalist]
- SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdullah Anik Misra, himself a convert) says honest mistakes made before learning, including accidentally acting on another school's ruling, are not sins. Converts should stay with one school and learn gradually. [SeekersGuidance: How can a convert follow a school correctly](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/how-can-a-convert-follow-a-school-of-thought-correctly/)

**3. Not obligatory. Follow trustworthy scholars, and the locally dominant school is a sensible default.**
- The **European Council for Fatwa and Research** (via IslamOnline): following a particular madhhab is not a religious obligation. Converts may follow any of the four, or the fatwa of a trusted scholar. Choosing the school dominant where you live avoids alienation from the community. [IslamOnline: New converts, which madhhab](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/new-converts-which-madhhab-to-follow/) [multi-madhab, Qaradawi-affiliated]
- **IslamQA.info** (Shaykh al-Munajjid): following a specific madhhab is not obligatory. A layperson should ask scholars known for knowledge and piety, and follow the evidence wherever it lies. [IslamQA.info 21420](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/21420) [Salafi-leaning]
- **Yasir Qadhi** (as transcribed from a 2013 lecture): a layperson should find an accessible, pious, knowledgeable scholar and follow them. Allah will not punish a layperson for being Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki or non-madhhab. Communities should not split over issues like raising the hands. [Google Groups forwarded transcript](https://groups.google.com/g/PPR_MUSLIMS/c/qw9BlE-Y7kI) — **medium confidence** (upgraded by the fact-check on 2026-10-05: the lecture is "Which Madhhab should you follow?", recorded 4 Jan 2013 in London and hosted on several lecture sites; the transcript matches its date and content. The video itself was not watched.)

**Common ground across all three views (high confidence):** a convert should not try to work out rulings personally from hadith; should learn from a qualified, accessible teacher; and should not let madhhab differences cause conflict at the masjid. All four schools accept praying behind an imam of another school. Sources: SeekersGuidance (above), IslamQA.info 21420, ECFR/IslamOnline, [AMJA 78081](https://www.amjaonline.org/fatwa/en/78081/wiping-on-the-socks).

**What the views disagree on:** whether choosing a madhhab is *obligatory* (view 1) or only *recommended / one valid option* (views 2–3), and whether mixing opinions across schools (talfiq / takhayyur) is allowed for laypeople. → Scholar review item.

## Questions for scholar review

1. **Default prayer method.** Which school's method should lessons teach by default at the pilot masjid: the imam's school, a "common practice" with notes, or a choice of school at onboarding? The schools differ on hand placement, raising the hands, basmala, Fatiha behind the imam, and the tashahhud finger.
2. **Choosing a madhab.** Should the curriculum tell converts to follow one madhab (traditionalist view), recommend the masjid's prevailing practice, or present "follow a trusted local scholar" (ECFR / IslamQA view)? What wording?
3. **Ghusl on conversion.** Teach it as obligatory (Hanbali/Maliki) or as recommended, and obligatory only after prior janaba or menstruation (Hanafi/Shafi'i)? In practice, should the shahada lesson simply tell every convert to do ghusl before their first prayer?
4. **Before Fatiha is memorized.** Should the app give the dhikr substitute (subhan Allah, al-hamdu lillah…), a short surah (al-Ikhlas), or both? Is a transliteration card on screen during prayer acceptable, given that the Hanafi view holds reading from a written text in prayer can invalidate it?
5. **Translations in prayer.** Should a translation of Fatiha ever be offered for recitation in prayer (minority Hanafi report), or only for understanding outside prayer?
6. **Wudu nullifiers.** Which position to teach on touching the opposite sex and on bleeding, especially for converts in healthcare and other jobs with physical contact?
7. **Socks.** Should wiping over ordinary socks be taught (AMJA), restricted to thick or leather socks (classical schools), or left out of week one?
8. **Meat.** Should lessons say "eat only zabiha/certified halal", present AMJA's People-of-the-Book view as valid, or present both? Is a convert's family dinner (non-zabiha meat, no pork) a special case?
9. **Holidays.** How should Christmas, Thanksgiving, birthdays and church weddings or funerals with family be presented? The range runs from attending the social parts and greeting (SeekersGuidance, Dar al-Ifta Egypt) to not participating or greeting at all (IslamQA.info, AMJA 2006).
10. **Gradualism wording.** How should the app phrase "you are still learning X" so that it encourages without implying an obligation is suspended (IslamWeb 529861 vs. al-Qaradawi on hijab)? Is it acceptable for a lesson to say "start with what you can" about prayer?
11. **Hijab timing.** When and how should hijab be introduced for women converts: week one, later, or one-on-one with a female mentor?
12. **Handshakes.** Should the majority prohibition be taught with the Dar al-Ifta Egypt allowance mentioned for workplace situations, or should only the alternative greeting be taught?
13. **Mid-Ramadan conversion.** Teach refraining for the rest of the conversion day as obligatory (IslamQA.info) or recommended (Shafi'i)? Teach making up that day as sunnah?
14. **Combinable prayers on the conversion day.** If someone converts in Asr time, do they also owe Dhuhr? Or should the app simply teach "start with the next prayer"?
15. **Zakat start.** Confirm the hawl starts at conversion and nothing is owed for prior years, and decide whether zakat appears in month one or later.
16. **Isha end time.** Teach "until Fajr (avoid delaying past midnight)" or "until midnight"? This matters for converts with late work shifts.
17. **Witr.** Teach it as wajib (Hanafi) or as a strong sunnah, and as 3 rak'ahs or 1?
