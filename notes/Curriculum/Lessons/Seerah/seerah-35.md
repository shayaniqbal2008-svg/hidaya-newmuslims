## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- In 9 AH, in intense heat, he led a large army to Tabuk to meet a Byzantine threat; no battle took place; it was his last campaign [IslamQA 11575; IslamQA 305386; the heat and long journey also in Bukhari 4418]. Say "a large army" (30,000 is sirah only). No battle, but the Day 71 frame still applies to questions about fighting. `[SCHOLAR: Q39]`
- Ka'b ibn Malik (who had been at the pledge of 'Aqabah) stayed behind with no excuse: he had never been stronger or wealthier, owned two camels for the first time, and kept putting off preparing until the army was gone [Bukhari 4418].
- On the Prophet's ﷺ return, over eighty men who had stayed behind made excuses and swore oaths; he accepted their outward word, asked forgiveness for them, and left their hearts to Allah. Ka'b, a fluent speaker, told the truth: he had no excuse. The Prophet ﷺ said he had told the truth and to wait for Allah's decision [Bukhari 4418].
- Ka'b, Murarah ibn al-Rabi' and Hilal ibn Umayyah were not spoken to for fifty nights; even Ka'b's cousin Abu Qatadah didn't return his greeting; a letter from the king of Ghassan invited Ka'b to join him, and he burned it; on the fiftieth morning after Fajr a man called the good news from the hill of Sal'; Ka'b fell in prostration, gave the messenger his clothes, and the Prophet ﷺ, his face shining, told him it was his best day since his mother bore him [Bukhari 4418].
- Allah forgave the Prophet ﷺ, the Muhajirun and the Ansar, and the three who were left behind, turning to them so they could repent [Q 9:117–118]. Ka'b resolved to speak only the truth for the rest of his life [Bukhari 4418].

**Likely questions**
- *"Why were they boycotted? Do Muslims shun people today?"* It was a specific, temporary discipline by the Prophet ﷺ himself, pending revelation, and it ended in forgiveness and joy. It is not a model for believers shunning each other today. Don't encourage the convert to shun anyone; questions about cutting people off go to the imam.
- *"Who were the excuse makers?"* The hadith calls them people who stayed behind and swore excuses; the Qur'an (Surah al-Tawbah) speaks of hypocrites. Don't label anyone today a hypocrite.
- *"I've slipped. How do I come back?"* Warmly: struggling isn't leaving Islam. Tawbah is honest regret, stopping, and returning; Allah accepts repentance [Q 9:118]. Point to Day 62 and the welcome-back modules. Suggest one small step and the mentor. Never shame.
- *"What is tawbah?"* Turning back to Allah: admit it, regret it, stop, and resolve to do better; then keep going.

**Escalate:** a convert who sounds hopeless, ashamed to the point of crisis, or considering leaving Islam (mentor; safety rule if needed); questions about shunning or excommunication (imam).

**Don't**
- Don't use the boycott to suggest Allah or the community will reject a struggling convert.
- Don't present shunning as something Muslims do to each other today.
- Don't call anyone a hypocrite.
