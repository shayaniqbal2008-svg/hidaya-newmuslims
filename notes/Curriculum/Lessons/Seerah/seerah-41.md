## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- Anas served him for ten years and was never once told "uff" or "why did you do that / why didn't you" [Bukhari 6038].
- He never struck a woman or a servant (except in battle) and never took revenge for himself [Muslim 2328; Bukhari 3560].
- He never spoke obscenely and said the best are those with the best character [Bukhari 3559]. Aisha said his character was the Qur'an [Muslim 746]. "And indeed, you are of a great moral character" [Q 68:4].
- He served his family at home [Bukhari 676]; he played kindly with Anas's little brother about his pet bird [Bukhari 6203].
- At a graveyard he said he longed to see his brothers: those who had not yet come; he would recognise them by the marks of wudu on their faces, arms and legs [Muslim 249].
- None truly believes until he loves the Prophet ﷺ more than his father, his children and all people [Bukhari 15]. Love of Allah means following him [Q 3:31]. Allah and His angels send blessings on him, and believers are told to [Q 33:56]; he taught the words (the Salat Ibrahimiyya) [Bukhari 3370; Days 26–27].
- He was sent as a mercy to the worlds [Q 21:107]; what burdens believers grieves him [Q 9:128]; he is an excellent pattern [Q 33:21].
- Life outline for the timeline: born about 570 [IslamQA 147601]; revelation at forty [Bukhari 3902]; hijra after thirteen years, 622 CE, year 1 of the calendar [Bukhari 3902; Bukhari 3934]; died at sixty-three in 632 CE [Muslim 2348; Yaqeen].

**Likely questions**
- *"How do I send blessings on him?"* The fullest form is the Salat Ibrahimiyya they already say in prayer [Bukhari 3370; Days 26–27]. Outside prayer, short forms like "Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammad" (O Allah, send blessings on Muhammad), or writing/saying ﷺ after his name. No fixed number is required here; any amount is good.
- *"How can I love someone I've never met?"* The same way you come to love any great person: by learning about him, seeing how he treated people, and following what he taught. He longed for people like them [Muslim 249]. Love grows; it isn't a test they fail.
- *"Do I have to love him more than my parents? That feels wrong."* Gently: Bukhari 15 describes the highest love, and it grows over time. Loving him never means loving family less; he taught kindness to parents and family. Don't make it a guilt test (Day 15 rule).
- *"What should I read next?"* Suggest the mentor and imam recommend a seerah book in the convert's language; offer to revisit any episode.
- *"What were his everyday habits?"* Keep to sourced ones: serving his family [Bukhari 676], gentleness with children [Bukhari 6203; Bukhari 5997], clean speech [Bukhari 3559], never hitting [Muslim 2328], loving the siwak [Bukhari 4449]. Specific sunnah practices and their rulings go to the masjid's teachers.

**Escalate:** questions about celebrating the Mawlid or specific forms of praise (imam); a convert who feels unworthy to the point of despair (mentor).

**Don't**
- Don't say "the Prophet ﷺ would be disappointed in you" or use him to shame (Day 15 rule).
- Don't treat Bukhari 15 as a feelings test or a threat.
- Don't describe his physical appearance or suggest picturing him.
- Don't give rulings on numbers of salawat or on contested practices.
