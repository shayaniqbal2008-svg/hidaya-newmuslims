## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- His children: al-Qasim, 'Abdullah, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum and Fatimah, all with Khadijah; and Ibrahim (born 8 AH), whose mother was Mariyah [IslamQA 23294; Qalam ep. 22; Dar al-Ifta Egypt, seerah part 3; Bukhari 3818]. `[SCHOLAR: Q40]`
- All died in his lifetime except Fatimah, who died soon after him, the first of his family to follow him [IslamQA 23294; Bukhari 3624].
- He wept at Ibrahim's death and called it mercy [Bukhari 1303].
- Fatimah is part of him; whoever angers her angers him [Bukhari 3714]. He welcomed her as "my daughter" [Bukhari 3624].
- He kissed al-Hasan; al-Aqra' said he had ten children and had kissed none; he said whoever isn't merciful won't be shown mercy [Bukhari 5997].
- He married no one else while Khadijah lived [Muslim 2436], about twenty-five years; he was about fifty when she died [Yaqeen, *What Islam Says About Prophet Muhammad*]. Don't say he "remained unmarried for several years" after her (log 40.6).
- His wives are honoured as the Mothers of the Believers [Q 33:6]. The common count is eleven [Yaqeen; IslamQA 47072]: Khadijah, Sawdah, Aisha, Hafsah, Zaynab bint Khuzaymah, Umm Salamah, Zaynab bint Jahsh, Juwayriyah, Umm Habibah, Safiyyah and Maymunah. Some lists differ. `[SCHOLAR: Q41]`
- The best of you are the best to their families, and he was the best to his [Tirmidhi 3895]. He served his family at home [Bukhari 676].

**Likely questions**
- *"Why did he have so many wives?"* Calmly: he was married only to Khadijah for about twenty-five years, until he was about fifty; his later marriages were mostly to widows or divorcees, many tied to alliances between tribes and to caring for women who had lost husbands [Yaqeen]. Don't argue or speculate; offer "Ask my imam" for more. `[SCHOLAR: Q41]`
- *"How old was Aisha when she married him?"* Never on a screen. If asked: the sahih reports in Bukhari and Muslim give six at the marriage contract and nine when the marriage began [Bukhari 5133; IslamQA 122534]. She became one of the greatest scholars of Islam. Marriage norms in that time differed greatly from today's, and none of his many opponents objected to it. Some Muslim researchers argue for an older age [Yaqeen, *Aisha: The Case for an Older Age*], while others defend the narration [Yaqeen, *The Age of Aisha*]. Never deny the hadith, never mock the question, and offer the imam. `[SCHOLAR: Q37]`
- *"Who was Mariyah? Was she his wife?"* Ibrahim's mother; IslamQA describes her as a gift from the ruler of Egypt who became his concubine, and scholars differ on whether he married her. Say plainly that slavery and concubinage existed everywhere then; don't claim either is acceptable today; detailed questions go to the imam. `[SCHOLAR: Q40]`
- *"What about Zaynab bint Jahsh and his adopted son?"* AI guide only: she had been married to Zayd, whom he had raised as a son; after their divorce he married her, and Yaqeen's paper explains the Qur'an presenting the marriage as ending the old custom of treating adopted sons like biological sons. Point to Yaqeen's paper on the marriage and the imam; don't quote the verse (not in the Citation Bank yet). `[SCHOLAR: Q41]`
- *"How did he treat his wives?"* He served his family [Bukhari 676], never struck a woman [Muslim 2328], and said the best are the best to their families [Tirmidhi 3895].
- *"How can I be gentler with my family?"* Small daily habits: affection, thanks, help at home. If the convert's family is hostile, keep kindness without putting themselves at risk (safety rule).

**Escalate:** Aisha's age beyond the outline above (Q37); slavery or concubinage (Q40); polygyny, Zaynab or Safiyyah in detail (Q41); any unsafe home situation (safety rule).

**Don't**
- Don't put Aisha's age, Mariyah's status or "why so many wives" on a screen or raise them unprompted.
- Don't deny or weaken a sahih hadith, and don't mock a sincere question.
- Don't comment on the convert's own marriage choices.
