## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- In Shawwal 8 AH the Hawazin and Thaqif gathered against him at Hunayn [IslamQA 11575] (single organisation; "IslamQA reports").
- The Muslims were pleased with their great number, but it didn't avail them; they turned back fleeing; then Allah sent down His tranquility on His Messenger and the believers [Q 9:25–26].
- The Prophet ﷺ did not flee; when hasty people ran from the Hawazin archers, he stayed on his white mule (held by Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith), calling out that he is the Prophet, no lie, the son of 'Abd al-Muttalib [Bukhari 4315].
- He besieged Ta'if but withdrew; its people later accepted Islam [IslamQA 11575; IslamQA 305386].
- The Hawazin came to him as Muslims; he had waited for them over ten days; offered their property or their captives, they chose their families; he asked the Muslims to return them freely, and after their chiefs checked with everyone, all gave up their shares willingly [Bukhari 2307]. `[SCHOLAR: Q40]`
- He gave large gifts to new Muslims "whose hearts were being reconciled" and none to the Ansar; they felt hurt; he reminded them how Allah had guided, united and enriched them through him, asked if they weren't pleased to go home with the Prophet while others took sheep and camels, said that but for the hijra he'd have been one of the Ansar, and that he would take their valley [Bukhari 4330]. Note: Bukhari 4330 doesn't mention the Ansar weeping (the early biographers do); don't attribute tears to this hadith.
- "Bringing hearts together" is one of the uses of zakat named in the Qur'an [Q 9:60]; Day 46 covers zakat.

**Likely questions**
- *"What happened to the captives?"* Say plainly that taking captives in war was universal at the time. The episode shows the families returned freely when the Hawazin came as Muslims [Bukhari 2307]. Don't discuss the division of captives. Don't claim slavery is acceptable today or that Islam abolished it in one stroke; detailed questions go to the imam. `[SCHOLAR: Q40]`
- *"Why give gifts to new Muslims and not to loyal ones?"* The Qur'an names reconciling hearts as a use of zakat [Q 9:60]. New Muslims were helped to settle into faith; the Ansar were trusted to want more than wealth. Converts today are among those Muslims are encouraged to support.
- *"Why fight at all?"* Keep to the history: the tribes gathered against him. No rulings on fighting today (Day 71 frame). `[SCHOLAR: Q39]`
- *"I feel overlooked at my masjid."* Listen first. Being overlooked is real and common for converts. Encourage a gentle word to the right person, the mentor's help, and reminding themselves their service is seen by Allah (Day 62 on intention). Never blame the convert.

**Escalate:** detailed questions on captives or slavery (Q40); fighting today (imam); a convert being excluded or mistreated at a masjid (mentor and dashboard escalation).

**Don't**
- Don't describe the fighting or casualties.
- Don't say the Ansar wept "in Bukhari".
- Don't turn the episode into criticism of the convert's masjid.
