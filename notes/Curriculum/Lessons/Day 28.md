## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- The Hadith of Jibril frames the religion: Islam (five pillars), iman (six articles), ihsan (worship as though you see Him) [Muslim 8 (Umar's narration, with qadar); Bukhari 50 (Abu Hurayra's narration)].
- Five pillars: shahada, salah, zakat, fasting Ramadan, Hajj for those able. Six articles: Allah, angels, books, messengers, the Last Day, qadar (good and bad).
- Allah is the Arabic word for God; Muslims believe He is the God of Abraham, Moses and Jesus, though Muslims and Christians understand Him differently (Day 27).
- Prayer phrase: *Rabbana atina…* [Q 2:201], the Prophet's ﷺ most frequent du'a [Bukhari 6389]. In the Hanafi manual it's given as an example of the du'a in Arabic in the last sitting before the salam [First Instructions on Worship]. Personal du'as in English belong outside the prayer (Hanafi).
- Days 31–41 go deeper on each article. Keep today at the "plain words" level.

**Likely questions**
- *"Is Allah the same God?"* Muslims believe there is only one God, the God of Abraham; "Allah" is simply the Arabic word, used by Arab Christians too. Beliefs about Him differ (e.g. the Trinity). Say it gently.
- *"How do I explain qadar?"* "Nothing happens outside God's knowledge; we still choose and make an effort." Day 43 goes deeper.
- *"Why five times a day?"* It's what Allah commanded; Muslims experience it as staying connected all day.
- *"Can I make du'a in English in the prayer?"* Hanafi: in the prayer, du'as are in Arabic, using wording like the Qur'an's or the Prophet's ﷺ; make personal du'as in any language before or after. Other schools differ somewhat; ask the imam.

**Escalate:** none specific; deep theology questions can go to Days 31–43 or the imam.

**Don't**
- Don't turn the 30-second answer into a lecture.
- Don't compare Islam favorably by putting other religions down.

**Role-play brief:** Aaliyah is curious and kind, asks simple follow-ups. Debrief on clarity, brevity, explaining terms.
