## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- Allah's names are the most beautiful; call on Him by them [Q 7:180]. Allah has ninety-nine names; whoever "counts" them enters Paradise [Bukhari 7392]. Scholars explain "counting" as learning, understanding and living by them [Revealed Path ch. 8].
- Today's six: ar-Razzaq, the Provider [Q 51:58]; at-Tawwab, who accepts repentance again and again [Q 49:12]; as-Salam, Peace, free of every flaw [Q 59:23]; al-Hakim, the Wise [Q 59:24]; al-Latif, the Subtle and Gently Kind [Q 42:19; Yusuf's words in Q 12:100]; ash-Shakur, the Appreciative [Q 35:30].
- Day 6 covered ar-Rahman, ar-Rahim, al-Ghafur, al-Wadud, al-Qarib, al-Mujib. "Allah" is His proper name, used for Him alone [Revealed Path ch. 8].
- Names describe one God. Nothing is like Him [Q 42:11]; comparisons only go so far.
- Knowing a name should shape character: knowing He is merciful makes you merciful; knowing He hears makes you careful with words [Revealed Path ch. 8; Being Muslim, Belief].

**Likely questions**
- *"Where's the full list?"* Lists of ninety-nine are widely circulated. The most famous list is a scholar's compilation, and scholars have compiled the names somewhat differently, but all agree on the core names in the Qur'an. Suggest learning a few at a time with meanings; their mentor or a class can help.
- *"Are there exactly ninety-nine?"* Allah has ninety-nine names with this special promise [Bukhari 7392]; scholars say He has others beyond them. Keep it short.
- *"Can I name a child 'Abdur-Razzaq' or 'Rahman'?"* Names like Abdur-Rahman ("servant of the Merciful") are common and loved. Naming someone with a name that belongs only to Allah (like "ar-Rahman" alone) is avoided. Details: ask the imam.
- *"Why so many names if He's one?"* Each describes a real attribute of the one God; we know Him better through each. Offer the "many words, one person" image, and its limit [Q 42:11]. Avoid sun or water analogies (they echo Trinity illustrations).
- *"Explaining to a Christian friend?"* Both traditions describe God as merciful, loving, forgiving. Keep it about what you believe, kindly.
- *"Should I say the names in Arabic?"* Arabic is beautiful and worth learning, but du'a is accepted in any language.

**Escalate:** none specific.

**Don't**
- Don't get into creed debates about Allah's attributes (interpretation of His "hand," "face," etc.). Say scholars have discussed these carefully and the core is that nothing is like Him [Q 42:11].
- Don't present any list of ninety-nine as Qur'an or as the Prophet's own words.
- Don't promise that saying a name a certain number of times guarantees a specific result.
