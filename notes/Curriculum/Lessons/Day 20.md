## AI guide context
**Lesson facts**
- Greeting: as-salamu 'alaykum; reply wa 'alaykum as-salam; returning a greeting is required and one may answer with better [Q 4:86]; greeting known and unknown people is a fine deed [Bukhari 12]; each added part brings more reward [Abu Dawud 5195, sahih per al-Albani].
- Bismillah before eating [Bukhari 5376]; alhamdulillah after sneezing, with the reply *yarhamuk Allah* and its answer [Bukhari 6224]; in sha Allah for future plans [Q 18:23–24]; masha Allah when admiring [Q 18:39].
- Ta'awwudh before reciting [Q 16:98]; wording *a'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim* [Bukhari 6115]. Hanafi: ta'awwudh once, in the first rak'ah (and when a latecomer starts making up); basmala quietly before al-Fatiha in every rak'ah; basmala isn't counted a verse of al-Fatiha [First Instructions on Worship ch. 7; Research Notes - Core Essentials and Madhab Map].
- These phrases are habits of remembrance, not requirements to belong; plain English is fine [Field Guide ch. 2; Welcome to Islam glossary].

**Likely questions**
- *"Can I say salam to non-Muslims?"* Scholars differ on starting the Islamic greeting with non-Muslims; a warm "hello" or "good morning" is always fine; if a non-Muslim says salam to you, reply kindly. Offer "Ask my imam" for more.
- *"What about sneezing?"* Say alhamdulillah; the listener says *yarhamuk Allah* (may Allah have mercy on you); you answer *yahdikumullah wa yuslih balakum* (may Allah guide you and set your affairs right) [Bukhari 6224].
- *"Isn't it weird to say these at work?"* Not required at all; use them when natural. In sha Allah in English is "God willing."
- *"Other phrases?"* JazakAllahu khayran (may Allah reward you with good: a thank-you), astaghfirullah (I ask Allah's forgiveness), Allahu akbar. Briefly.

**Escalate:** none specific.

**Don't**
- Don't make the convert feel less Muslim for not using Arabic.
- Don't call saying "Merry Christmas" or holiday greetings settled either way (Q5 territory; route to the imam).

## Madhab note
- **Basmala before al-Fatiha:** Hanafis and Hanbalis say it quietly and don't count it as a verse of al-Fatiha; Shafi'is count it as a verse and say it aloud in the prayers recited aloud; Malikis don't say it before al-Fatiha in obligatory prayers.
- **Ta'awwudh:** Hanafis say it once in the first rak'ah; some other schools repeat it in each rak'ah.
