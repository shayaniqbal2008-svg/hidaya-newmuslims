# Research Notes: Books and Written Guides for New Muslims

Agent A (books and written guides). Researched 2026-10-05 with web search and web fetch, using only legitimately free sources: author and publisher pages, free official sites, reviews, Goodreads, Google Books metadata, and archive.org metadata. No pirated uploads were opened. Several pirate or gray-market mirrors (dokumen.pub, noor-book, Scribd uploads) came up in search results and were deliberately skipped.

Everything below is summarized in my own words. Where a source is paywalled and its table of contents was not visible, the entry says so. Nothing here is a religious ruling. These notes record what each source teaches and in what order.

## Sources

### Welcome to Islam: A Step-by-Step Guide for New Muslims
- **Author/org:** Mustafa Umar. American imam and author who runs mustafaumar.com; Sunni. His site lists other practical titles such as *How to Pray* and a Spanish edition, *Bienvenidos al Islam*. I did not verify his formal credentials in this session.
- **Format & length:** Short booklet, about 32 to 48 pages depending on the listing (author site: 32 pp; Google Books: 46 pp; Goodreads Kindle: 48 pp). Published 2011 through CreateSpace. An Audible audiobook edition also exists.
- **Intended audience:** People who have just accepted Islam. The author describes it as answering the "Ok, now what?" question.
- **Topics in order:** **Could not get the full TOC.** The author's "read free online" link opens an old Flash flipbook (mustafaumar.com/files/flipbook/welcometoislam/) that no longer renders. The archive.org copy is borrow-only, and Amazon "Look Inside" did not load for me. From the author's description and reviews, the topics are as follows (order is **not** confirmed):
  1. Shahadah (declaring faith)
  2. The Five Pillars, as an overview
  3. Wudu and salah, taught one step at a time
  4. Daily practices
  5. Dress code
  6. Islamic greetings
  7. Halal food basics
  8. Common problems new Muslims face (the author's page mentions "practical guidance on common challenges")
- **Pacing:** Explicitly a **first-month program**. The author says it covers the basics a new Muslim should learn in their first month. The book is meant to be practiced as you go, step by step, and is not just a reference. Reviewers say you can read it in 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Tone:** Plain, practical, instruction-manual style.
- **Strengths:** Very short. Action-oriented ("start practicing what you learn immediately"). Reviewers say it works especially well for converts in rural areas with no community nearby. Low price ($3.99), and the author once offered it free online.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Goodreads reviewers wanted Qur'an and hadith references, at least in footnotes, and found the prayer instructions thin. It is too short to cover family, marriage, or social issues in any depth.
- **How accessed:** The author's book page (description and metadata), Goodreads description and reviews, Google Books metadata, and archive.org metadata (borrow-only, not opened). I did not see the TOC.
- **URL(s):** https://mustafaumar.com/downloads/welcome-to-islam/ ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18987447-welcome-to-islam ; https://archive.org/details/welcometoislamst0000umar ; https://www.amazon.com/Welcome-Islam-Step-Step-Muslims/dp/1461104777
- **Confidence:** Medium for metadata and positioning. Low for topic order.

### The New Muslim's Field Guide
- **Author/org:** Theresa Corbin and Kaighla Um Dayo, both American converts and writers (Corbin runs islamwich.com). AboutIslam's review notes they are "not Islamic scholars." Sunni.
- **Format & length:** Paperback, about 216 to 218 pages, **18 chapters**, 2018. Originally CreateSpace; Goodreads lists Daybreak Press. It won the 2019 Daybreak Press Book Award for Best Non-fiction Islamic Book.
- **Intended audience:** Western converts in their first months and years. Reviewers say it also helps heritage Muslims and interfaith families.
- **Topics in order:** **Full chapter titles not found.** Pieced together from three reviews, in the order the reviewers describe:
  1. Two opening chapters on culture vs. religion: you do not stop being yourself, and Islam is not Arab or South Asian culture
  2. Practical identity questions: whether to change your name, Arabic terms, clothing
  3. Telling and dealing with non-Muslim family
  4. Hijab, including wearing it in public
  5. Going to the masjid for the first time
  6. Praying outside the home
  7. Islamophobia (and, per Goodreads reviewers, extremism)
  8. Six pillars of faith (an information-heavy chapter)
  9. Five pillars of action, including prayer and Ramadan (an information-heavy chapter)
  10. Manners and etiquette
  11. Opposite-sex relationships
  12. Sex and marriage
  13. Marriage chapters for sisters and for brothers: heritage vs. convert spouse, how soon to marry, marriage contract, rights, and a "deal breakers" list
  14. Glossary and recommended reading
- **Pacing:** Reference book or orientation read, not a sequenced program. Reviewers praise it for putting spiritual foundation before rules.
- **Tone:** Warm, conversational, humorous, like "sitting with my dearest friend, drinking coffee" (AboutIslam review). One Goodreads reviewer found it a bit too jokey.
- **Strengths:** Covers the **social and identity side** that most guides skip: family, name, culture, masjid culture shock, marriage, Islamophobia. Written by converts for converts. Honest about the hard parts.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Readers note it is **not a how-to manual**, with little step-by-step instruction on prayer or fasting and little depth on theology. It is not by scholars. Some found it too humorous.
- **How accessed:** Reviews on AboutIslam and The Dua Journal, plus Goodreads description and reviews. Amazon and Google Books did not show a TOC.
- **URL(s):** https://aboutislam.net/reading-islam/research-studies/the-new-muslims-field-guide-book-review-2/ ; https://theduajournal.com/blogs/news/resource-review-the-new-muslims-field-guide ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/38357539-the-new-muslim-s-field-guide ; https://islamwich.com/2018/02/01/the-new-muslims-field-guide/
- **Confidence:** High for metadata and topic coverage. Medium-low for exact order.

### The New Muslim Guide (newmuslimguide.com)
- **Author/org:** Fahd Salem Bahammam. Published by the Contemporary Guide Company (al-Dalil al-Mu'asir), Saudi-based. The site's social handles are "WRMuslims." Sunni, with a mainstream Gulf/Salafi-leaning presentation.
- **Format & length:** A free website in more than 26 languages, plus a mobile app and a printed illustrated handbook. It is organized as a preliminaries section with 13 short topics, then **12 "Your ___" categories**, each holding several topics. The printed edition runs about 300 pages (my recollection from bookstore listings; not verified this session).
- **Intended audience:** New converts worldwide. Also used by da'wah organizations for handouts.
- **Topics in order:** (From the live site. Subtopics were scraped from the Arabic default view and translated by me.)
  - **Preliminaries:**
    1. The greatest blessing (guidance)
    2. The purpose of existence
    3. Islam as a universal religion
    4. No intermediary between a person and God
    5. Islam as a religion for all of life
    6. Learning the rulings
    7. Categories of legal rulings
    8. The Five Pillars
    9. How to find out a ruling
    10. Moderation
    11. Religion covers every part of life
    12. Judge Islam by its teachings, not by Muslims' behavior
    13. The five necessities (maqasid)
  1. **Your Faith:** the meaning of the shahadah ("no god but God") and its conditions; knowing the Prophet; belief in God, including worship and shirk; then belief in angels, books, and the other articles of faith
  2. **Your Purification:** the meaning of taharah; removing impurity and toilet etiquette; minor impurity and wudu (how to do wudu); major impurity and ghusl; tayammum
  3. **Your Prayer:** status and virtues of prayer; who must pray; conditions; the five prayers and their times; place of prayer; how to pray; pillars, obligations, and invalidators; voluntary prayers; congregational prayer; adhan and iqamah; khushu' (focus); Jumu'ah, including whether work is an excuse; prayer while traveling; prayer while ill
  4. **Your Fast:** Ramadan and its virtues; the wisdom of fasting; what breaks a fast; who is excused; voluntary fasts; Eid al-Fitr
  5. **Your Zakaat:** purposes; which wealth is zakatable; who receives it
  6. **Your Pilgrimage:** Makkah; who must perform Hajj; ability to go; the mahram requirement for women; virtues and purposes; Umrah; Eid al-Adha and udhiyah; visiting Madinah
  7. **Your Financial Transactions:** the default that dealings are permitted; riba (including converts with existing interest contracts); gharar (uncertainty); unjust gain (including money taken before Islam); gambling; trust, honesty, and doing good work
  8. **Your Food and Drink:** the default that food is permitted; crops; alcohol; drugs; seafood; permitted animals and slaughter, including meat in restaurants; hunting; eating etiquette
  9. **Your Dress Code:** purposes of clothing; the default and prohibited clothing
  10. **Your Family:** status of the family; women in Islam and limits between unrelated men and women; marriage conditions; spouses' rights; polygyny; divorce; parents' rights; children's rights
  11. **Your Moral Character:** status of character; character even in war; humility, mercy, justice, generosity in the Prophet's life
  12. **Your New Life:** how one enters Islam and the convert's ghusl; repentance; gratitude for guidance; da'wah, including inviting family; **your environment and family**; when one spouse converts and the other does not, and children's status; whether to change one's name; the fitrah practices
- **Pacing:** A reference organized by topic, not a daily program. The order follows the classical fiqh-manual pattern (creed, purification, prayer, fasting, zakat, hajj, transactions, food, dress, family), with convert-specific "new life" material at the **end**.
- **Tone:** Formal, rule-focused, illustrated, with many evidences cited.
- **Strengths:** The most **complete topic map** of any source. Free, multilingual, app-ready. Includes convert-specific rulings that other guides skip: existing interest contracts, money gained before Islam, one spouse converting, name change, restaurant meat.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Heavy on rulings and light on emotional and social support. The convert's lived experience (family, loneliness, community) comes last. The presentation is mostly a single school/Gulf approach, and US-specific issues (holidays, workplace, school) are thin. I found no published reviews.
- **How accessed:** Full free website. Category pages were fetched directly and subtopic titles extracted.
- **URL(s):** https://www.newmuslimguide.com/en ; https://www.newmuslimguide.com/en/preliminaries ; https://www.newmuslimguide.com/en/categories/your-new-life
- **Confidence:** High for section order. Medium for the subtopic translations, which are my own renderings from Arabic.

### New Muslims eLearning (newmuslims.com) / IslamReligion.com New Muslims material
- **Author/org:** Run by "Distinguished Content for Communications and IT Est." with the Cooperative Office for Dawah in Rawdah (Riyadh). The lessons are largely articles from IslamReligion.com authors. Sunni, Saudi da'wah-office material.
- **Format & length:** An online course with **10 levels**. Level 1 has 23 lessons; levels have roughly 20 to 30+ lessons each, with a quiz after each lesson and a review test after each level. Free, with an Android app.
- **Intended audience:** Recent converts who want a systematic path.
- **Topics in order:** Level 1 order confirmed:
  1. The Testimony of Faith
  2. Introduction to the Pillars of Islam and Articles of Faith (2 parts)
  3. Common questions from recent converts
  4. Importance of seeking knowledge
  5. Paradise (2 parts)
  6. The Night Journey
  7. How to pray, for a recent convert (2 parts)
  8. **Breaking the news to family (2 parts)**
  9. Adjusting to the Muslim community
  10. Keeping good company
  11. Belief in Allah: tawhid and shirk (2 parts)
  12. Belief in prophets
  13. Belief in scriptures
  14. Belief in angels
  15. Belief in the Day of Judgment
  16. Divine decree (2 parts)
  17. Study methodology for a new Muslim

  Level 2 onward, as the site's sequential lesson menu shows them: meeting Muhammad; preservation of the Qur'an; importance of prayer; ghusl; wudu; prayer for beginners (2 parts); benefits of prayer; toilet manners; menstruation; dietary laws; the Muslim family; love of God; fasting, how to fast, and Eid; "Where is Allah?"; Abraham; Al-Fatiha and short surahs; a beginner's guide to the Qur'an and to hadith. Later levels cover detailed prayer (2, 3, and 4 units); a day in the life of a Muslim; repentance; the Sunnah; eating etiquette; Ayat al-Kursi; travel prayers; omens and amulets; adhan; shirk; tayammum; sects; protection from evil; purification of the soul; dress code; Satan; du'a; God's mercy; role models; **trials and tribulations after conversion**; mosque manners; good habits; Friday prayer; prophets' stories; marriage advice; spouses' rights.
- **Pacing:** A **sequential program** with gated levels and quizzes. This is the closest existing analogue to a structured curriculum.
- **Tone:** Simple and article-based, with occasional apologetics such as the "medical benefits of prayer."
- **Strengths:** Deliberate order. It puts shahadah, an overview, and **how to pray and how to tell family** in the very first unit, before the detailed creed. It has assessment built in and a free mobile app. It spirals: prayer, wudu, and ghusl come back at deeper levels.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Long, with 10 levels and 200+ lessons. Some content (omens, amulets, sects) suits a particular theological emphasis. The material is article-based, not designed for mobile micro-lessons. The US cultural context is thin. I found no independent reviews; a 2012 MuslimMatters post recommends it as a learning path.
- **How accessed:** Free website. The Level 1 page was fetched directly and the lesson menu scraped.
- **URL(s):** https://www.newmuslims.com/ ; https://www.newmuslims.com/level/1 ; https://www.newmuslims.com/lessons/150/study-methodology-for-new-muslim ; https://muslimmatters.org/2012/08/31/new-muslim-series-learning-path-through-the-basics-of-islam-online-curriculum/
- **Confidence:** High for the Level 1 order. Medium for the exact level boundaries after Level 1.

### A Brief Illustrated Guide to Understanding Islam (islam-guide.com)
- **Author/org:** I. A. Ibrahim. Edited with input from several scholars and scientists; 1996/97, Darussalam. Sunni da'wah literature. The site itself did not name the author in what I fetched; the attribution comes from my background knowledge of this widely distributed booklet, so treat it as medium confidence.
- **Format & length:** A short illustrated booklet of about 70 pages in print, in three chapters. Free website and PDF.
- **Intended audience:** **Non-Muslims who are curious**, not new Muslims. It is a da'wah tract.
- **Topics in order:**
  1. **Evidence for the truth of Islam:** scientific miracles in the Qur'an (embryology, mountains, origin of the universe, cerebrum, seas, deep seas, clouds, scientists' comments); the challenge to produce a chapter like the Qur'an; Biblical prophecies of Muhammad; Qur'anic prophecies; the Prophet's miracles; his simple life; Islam's growth
  2. **Benefits of Islam:** Paradise; salvation from Hell; happiness and inner peace; forgiveness of past sins
  3. **General information:** what Islam is; basic beliefs; sources (Qur'an, hadith); Judgment Day; how to become Muslim; what the Qur'an is about; who Muhammad was; Islam and science; Jesus; terrorism; human rights; women; family; the elderly; the Five Pillars; Islam in the US
- **Pacing:** A one-shot read.
- **Tone:** Apologetic and persuasive, with heavy use of scientific claims.
- **Strengths:** Free, short, and widely distributed in US masjids.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** **Not a new-Muslim curriculum.** It contains nothing on how to pray, wudu, or life after conversion. The "scientific miracles" approach is contested by many Muslim academics; that is a general known criticism, and I did not pull a specific review for it.
- **How accessed:** Full free site.
- **URL(s):** https://www.islam-guide.com/
- **Confidence:** High for structure. Medium for author attribution.

### Being Muslim: A Practical Guide
- **Author/org:** Asad Tarsin. Holds a BA in Islamic studies from the University of Michigan; studied with Shaykh Hamza Yusuf and Dr. Sherman Jackson; was curriculum director of the Deen Intensive Foundation for about 10 years; trained as an ER physician. Published by Sandala with a foreword by Hamza Yusuf. Sunni, traditional (Zaytuna/Deen Intensive circle).
- **Format & length:** Paperback, about 288 to 312 pages depending on the listing (Goodreads lists 370). 8 chapters plus 3 appendices. 2015.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims and "returning" (re-engaging) Muslims.
- **Topics in order:** (from the TOC on the Wardah Books and IslamicBookstore listings)
  1. **Starting Point:** The Beginning; Islam in Context; Learning Islam
  2. **Belief:** The Six Pillars (shahadah, God, angels, scriptures, messengers, Last Day, decree)
  3. **Worship:** The Five Pillars (prayer, zakah, fasting Ramadan, hajj); Other Components of Worship; Rulings Particular to Women. Legal-ruling categories are introduced first.
  4. **Spiritual Refinement:** Human Consciousness; Path of Refinement; Destructive Vices; Saving Virtues; Noble Character
  5. **The Prophet Muhammad:** his role; introduction; salawat (sending blessings); brief biography; miracles
  6. **The Qur'an:** its nature; major themes
  7. **Islamic Holy Days:** the Islamic year; Friday prayer; Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr; Eid al-Adha
  8. **Lifestyle:** Living Islamic Law; Daily Life; Communal Life
  - Appendices: Qur'an selections; recommended reading; glossary
- **Pacing:** A sequential read that also works as a reference. It does not set a schedule, though Deen Intensive-style courses have used it as a textbook.
- **Tone:** Clear, warm, and traditional. It uses diagrams. The rulings largely follow the classical schools, with notes on where schools differ.
- **Strengths:** Readers often call it **the best single book for converts** (Goodreads 4.5/5, about 270 ratings). It balances practice with spirituality (a whole chapter on refinement of the heart), and the diagrams make the structure easy to follow.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Some Goodreads reviewers call it "information overload for a new Muslim." Another felt the fiqh chapters could confuse beginners on school differences without a teacher. Judging from the TOC, convert-specific social issues (telling family, holidays, loneliness, masjid culture) are folded into "Communal Life" at the end rather than given their own treatment. I have not seen inside that section.
- **How accessed:** TOC from the Wardah Books and IslamicBookstore.com listings; description and author bio from the Rabata bookshop; Goodreads reviews. The archive.org entry is a borrow-only scan and was not opened.
- **URL(s):** https://wardahbooks.com/products/beingmuslimapracticalguide ; https://www.islamicbookstore.com/13259.html ; https://bookshop.rabata.org/products/being-muslim ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/27860126-being-muslim
- **Confidence:** High for chapter and section order. Medium for page count.

### The Revealed Path: A Guide Book for New Muslims (ZHIC New Muslim Curriculum)
- **Author/org:** Hasan Lam (Parts I to III; Toronto teacher) and Ramzy Ajem (Part IV; founder of Risalah Foundation, Toronto, a new-Muslim services nonprofit; trained in Damascus and Morocco). Commissioned by the Zayed House for Islamic Culture (UAE), produced by Razi Group (Canada), and supervised by the Tabah Foundation. Sunni, traditional, with a companion volume for each of the four madhhabs.
- **Format & length:** Book of about 133 pages (PDF), 25 chapters in 4 parts. 2nd edition 2014. It is the "cornerstone" of a full curriculum that also includes *First Instructions on Worship* (in Maliki, Hanafi, Hanbali, and Shafi'i versions), a prayer DVD, prayer audio, a **teacher's manual**, and teacher training and certification.
- **Intended audience:** New Muslims in a taught program. It is also usable for self-study.
- **Topics in order:** (from the book's own contents page)
  - **Introduction:** a "travel guide" metaphor; advice for the journey; the Hadith of Gabriel as the organizing frame (islam, iman, ihsan)
  - **Part I, Islam:**
    1. Overview (the essence of Islam, the five pillars, the importance of knowledge, revealed law)
    2. Shahada (first steps, a new beginning)
    3. Prayer
    4. Zakat
    5. Fasting in Ramadan
    6. Hajj
  - **Part II, Iman:**
    7. Overview ("tree of faith")
    8. God (the divine names and attributes)
    9. Angels
    10. Revealed books, including the Qur'an in daily life
    11. Messengers, including sira and loving the Prophet
    12. Last Day
    13. Destiny
  - **Part III, Ihsan:**
    14. Overview
    15. Heart and limbs
    16. States of the heart (repentance, fear and hope, patience)
    17. **Company and surroundings**
    18. Dhikr
  - **Part IV, Rights and Responsibilities:**
    19. Rights of God and the Messenger
    20. The self (moderation, food, harmful substances, clothing)
    21. **Family relations** (marriage, children)
    22. Community of believers
    23. **Societal and civic duties: living in non-Muslim societies, interacting with other faiths**
    24. Financial interactions (work, debt, interest)
    25. Nature and the environment
  - The Journey Ahead; glossary; recommended reading
- **Pacing:** Designed as a **taught curriculum** with a teacher's manual, built on adult-education principles according to the ZHIC preface. The book gives the conceptual order, and the companion worship manual handles the step-by-step fiqh.
- **Tone:** Gentle and traditional, using a journey metaphor.
- **Strengths:** The **closest model to what this startup is building**: a deliberately designed new-Muslim curriculum with a teacher layer and per-madhhab practice manuals. It is organized around the Hadith of Gabriel, a classical frame that is easy to explain. It covers living as a minority, interfaith relations, and the environment.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** The practice comes before the creed (pillars first, then beliefs), and the how-to of prayer sits in a separate volume. It was written for a UAE and Canadian context; US-specific issues are not covered. I found no reviews.
- **How accessed:** A full PDF hosted by Beginnings (a UK new-Muslim charity). I read only the front matter and the contents page. The copyright page allows only fair use, and I did not confirm whether Beginnings' hosting is authorized by ZHIC.
- **URL(s):** https://beginnings.org.uk/downloads/English-E-Book-1-Guide-Book-for-New-Muslims.pdf ; publisher: http://www.zhic.ae
- **Confidence:** High for structure, which comes from the book's own TOC.

### Guide to Islam for New Muslims (Yahiya Emerick / IFNA)
- **Author/org:** Yahiya Emerick, an American convert and author of *The Complete Idiot's Guide to Understanding Islam* and a Qur'an translation. Published by the Islamic Foundation of North America (ifna.net). Sunni.
- **Format & length:** A free PDF of about 34 pages in 4 sections. Probably late 1990s to 2000s; the PDF is undated.
- **Intended audience:** New and potential Muslims who wrote in asking for information.
- **Topics in order:**
  1. How to become Muslim (the shahadah, with transliteration)
  2. **Section 1, Basic teachings:**
     - Beliefs (God, angels, books, messengers, Last Day, decree, afterlife)
     - Practices (shahadah, prayer with the five times, zakah, fasting, hajj)
     - Holidays
     - Halal and haram
     - Male/female relations
     - Names
     - Everyday Islamic phrases (about 15 phrases with meanings)
  3. **Section 2:** advice for new Muslims "on the path"
  4. **Section 3:** an annotated book list (Qur'an translations, introductions, sira, Arabic, hadith, spirituality, convert memoirs such as Jeffrey Lang, marriage, women, fiqh)
  5. **Section 4:** a glossary of terms
- **Pacing:** A one-shot primer and resource directory.
- **Tone:** Brief, plain, and somewhat dated.
- **Strengths:** American-authored. Short. Includes everyday phrases and names, which are practical social-integration items that bigger books skip. Its reading list is a good snapshot of the US convert canon.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Very thin on how to pray (no step-by-step). Dated references. Nothing on telling family, the workplace, or mental health.
- **How accessed:** A free PDF on islambasics.com (a free da'wah library). I read the text and headings.
- **URL(s):** https://islambasics.com/wp-content/uploads/Books/NewMuslim.pdf
- **Confidence:** High for the content. Low for the date.

### Islamic Studies, Book 1 (Abu Ameenah Bilal Philips)
- **Author/org:** Dr. Abu Ameenah Bilal Philips, a Canadian convert, PhD (University of Edinburgh), and founder of Islamic Online University (now International Open University). Published by IIPH. Sunni/Salafi. A Goodreads reviewer calls him "rather controversial."
- **Format & length:** About 200 pages, the first book of a 4-volume series (the author's site says 5). 2nd ed. 2005. Each unit ends with study questions.
- **Intended audience:** Mainly secondary students aged 12 to 18. The author's site and publisher **explicitly also name new Muslims** and adults wanting a simple explanation.
- **Topics in order:** **Full TOC not found.** The publisher says it is a downloadable file I could not reach. The publisher's description gives this unit order:
  1. Tawhid (monotheism)
  2. Qur'anic studies, with tafsir of selected surahs
  3. Hadith sciences
  4. Sources of fiqh (Islamic law)
- **Pacing:** A **textbook course** with units and review questions, meant to cover one school year per book.
- **Tone:** Academic and textbook-like, with evidence-based arguments.
- **Strengths:** Rigorous and structured, with built-in assessment. Many reverts on Goodreads report using it.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** It is not written for converts. It is discipline-based (aqidah, tafsir, hadith, fiqh) rather than life-based. There is little or nothing on the social side of conversion. One school of thought dominates.
- **Other Philips titles:** I found **no dedicated new-Muslim book by Bilal Philips.** His *The Fundamentals of Tawheed* and *The Evolution of Fiqh* are often recommended to converts, but they are subject studies.
- **How accessed:** The author's portfolio page, the IIPH product page, and Goodreads. An islamhouse.com free copy exists but returned an error (429) when I tried it.
- **URL(s):** https://bilalphilips.com/portfolio/islamic-studies-book-1/ ; https://iiph.com/product/islamic-studies-book-1/ ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/642719.Islamic_Studies_Book_1 ; https://islamhouse.com/en/books/273077/
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### Yaqeen Institute Convert Resources (and the unreleased "Yaqeen Basics")
- **Author/org:** Yaqeen Institute (Irving, TX), Convert Resources Department. Shaykh Abdullah Oduro, a convert, leads much of it. Sunni.
- **Format & length:** **No single written guide.** There is a hub page with short blog articles and video series. "Yaqeen Basics™" was announced on 30 July 2020 as an upcoming resource for "new and renewed Muslims," and the page still says "coming soon."
- **Intended audience:** New and "renewed" Muslims, convert coaches, and imams.
- **Topics in order:** (from the hub page)
  1. Pillar 4 (fasting) video series: intro, where, why, when, purpose, who should fast
  2. First-Ramadan reflections
  3. Connecting with yourself as a new Muslim in Ramadan
  4. Connecting with the Qur'an and Sunnah
  5. **Family and community during Ramadan**
  6. Connecting with Allah
  7. Also on the site: *5 Pillars Made Plain* (Oduro), the #ConvertStories series, the "Are we failing new Muslims?" podcast, and the research report **"Becoming Muslim: The Lives of American Converts to Islam"**
- **Pacing:** Not sequenced. It is a resource collection, with a heavy Ramadan focus.
- **Tone:** Warm and pastoral.
- **Strengths:** US-based, and it treats emotional and family issues. The "Becoming Muslim" report is likely the best **needs data** for a US curriculum and should go to the user-research agent.
- **Gaps / criticisms:** Fragmented. The structured product (Yaqeen Basics) never appears to have shipped, which is itself a market signal.
- **How accessed:** Free website pages.

## Cross-source observations

- **The shahadah almost always comes first, then an overview of the pillars.** Every convert-specific guide opens with the testimony of faith and what it means (Umar, newmuslims.com, WhyIslam, Zarabozo, Revealed Path Part I, Bahammam's "Your Faith," Emerick). Da'wah tracts aimed at non-Muslims (islam-guide.com) open with *evidence for truth* instead, which is a different job.
- **Two competing orders for the core.** (a) **Belief before practice:** Tarsin, Zarabozo, Bahammam, newmuslims.com, Wright, and Emerick go creed, then worship. (b) **Practice before belief:** The Revealed Path follows the Hadith of Gabriel order (islam, then iman, then ihsan), and Umar and WhyIslam jump to wudu and prayer almost immediately. newmuslims.com is a useful hybrid: it teaches *how to pray* and *telling family* in Level 1 **before** the detailed six articles, then revisits prayer in depth later (a spiral).
- **Prayer is the first practical skill everywhere,** usually taught in the order wudu, then (ghusl), then the prayer, in the first week or two. Fasting, zakat, and hajj follow, usually in pillar order regardless of season. Few sources schedule Ramadan content by the calendar; Yaqeen's material is the exception and is *all* Ramadan-themed.
- **A three-part spiritual layer (islam, iman, ihsan) appears in the traditional books** (Tarsin's "Spiritual Refinement," the Revealed Path's Part III, Rabbani's ihsan section, Zarabozo's "Fortifying faith"). Short guides and da'wah-office material mostly skip the inner life or reduce it to "good habits."
- **Social and identity issues are where sources split most.** The Field Guide leads with them (culture vs. religion, name, family, hijab, masjid shock, Islamophobia, marriage). newmuslims.com puts "breaking the news to family" and "adjusting to the community" in Level 1. Zarabozo and Bahammam cover them as rulings (non-Muslim relatives, pre-Islamic marriage and wealth, name change), but **late** in the book. Tarsin and the Revealed Path put them in a final "lifestyle" or "rights" part. Short guides (Umar, Emerick, WhyIslam, Rabbani) mostly omit them.
- **Commonly missing across almost all sources:** mental health, loneliness, and the faith-dip after conversion (only newmuslims.com's "Trials after conversion" and Zarabozo's "faith increases and decreases" touch it); **holidays with non-Muslim family** (Christmas, Thanksgiving); workplace and school accommodation in the US; how to **find a mentor** or community (only WhyIslam stresses it); avoiding being overwhelmed or pressured by the community (only the Field Guide); and **converts with existing debts or interest-bearing loans** (only Bahammam addresses it directly).
- **Typical length is bimodal.** "Quick start" guides run 30 to 50 pages and are read in one sitting (Umar, Emerick, WhyIslam, Rabbani, Darul Arqam). "Complete" guides run 200 to 300 pages (Tarsin, Field Guide, Zarabozo, Bahammam, Wright). Only **newmuslims.com** (10 levels, quizzes) and **The Revealed Path** (teacher's manual) are true sequenced programs. **No source I found is structured as daily lessons**, which is the gap this product fills.
- **The US convert canon is small and consistent.** WhyIslam's list (Bahammam, the Field Guide, Umar, plus *The Clear Quran*) overlaps heavily with this brief's must-cover list. Tarsin's *Being Muslim* is the most-praised single book among traditional teachers.
- **Madhhab handling varies.** Most US books present one view and do not name a school (Umar, Field Guide, Wright). Tarsin notes differences (some readers find that confusing). The Revealed Path solves it with **separate worship manuals per madhhab**, which is a useful model if masjid customers want their own school's fiqh.
- **Assessment is rare.** Only newmuslims.com (quizzes and level tests), Philips' textbook (unit questions), and the Darul Arqam catechism ("recite X") check competency. That is a differentiator worth borrowing for a daily app: short recall checks, plus "can you now recite Al-Fatiha?" skill checks.
