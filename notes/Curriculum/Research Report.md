## 1. Executive summary

**86 sources** were profiled: 18 books and written guides (5 read in full text), 20 programs and courses, 16 video series, 19 research studies and reports, ~32 individual convert voices, and Shayan's own Reddit research. Of these, 16 give a full topic sequence.

Seven findings shape everything downstream:

1. **Nothing on the market is built as daily lessons, and nothing combines a sequenced curriculum with a masjid layer.** Structured programs run 8–14 weekly sessions. Mentor programs have no curriculum. Self-paced sites (newmuslims.com, 10 levels) have no human layer. No program publishes completion or retention numbers. One masjid we profiled (Islamic Foundation North) refers converts to 3–5 outside providers because it has nothing in-house; that is our buyer's pain in one sentence.
2. **Every convert-specific source opens with the shahada and its meaning, then an overview of the pillars. Prayer is the first practical skill everywhere.** Where sources split is whether detailed belief (the six articles) comes *before* prayer (Tarsin, Zarabozo, Bahammam, iERA/Embrace, which don't reach salah until week 7–8) or *after* it (The Revealed Path, newmuslims.com, Mustafa Umar, WhyIslam). The evidence favors prayer early: Tier-1 scholars say all five prayers are due from day one, and the most-watched convert videos by a wide margin are step-by-step prayer videos.
3. **The risk period is social, not theological.** Loneliness is the single most common complaint (≈17 of 32 voices; 40% of converts feel lonely at Ramadan or Eid). Then conflicting instruction, then harsh correction. In Yaqeen's 2025 survey of 618 converts, the strongest predictors of staying strong in faith were Muslim friends, attachment to a mosque, and a capable imam. **The app must connect converts to people, not replace them.** That makes the masjid dashboard more than a progress tracker; it's the retention mechanism.
4. **Prayer should be taught in two layers.** The best video series teach the movements first with almost no Arabic, then add recitation one phrase per short lesson. Scholars across schools support this: until al-Fatiha is memorized, a convert says short phrases of dhikr (IslamQA 5410; Dar al-Ifta Egypt 8030) or a short verse they can manage (SeekersGuidance, Hanafi).
5. **Short daily beats long weekly.** Every video series with 30+ minute episodes loses most of its audience (one 13-week course fell from 1.3K views to 55). Yaqeen's 5-minute daily series held 20–30K per episode. A 5-minute daily lesson is the right unit.
6. **The two statistics in our pitch are wrong and must be replaced** (section 7). "20,000+ a year (Ta'leef)" traces to unnamed "observers" in a 2011 news story; the real number is **~31,000 a year, reported by mosques (US Mosque Survey 2020)**. "Many leave within two years" has no study behind it; the "75%" figure comes from an unpublished 1996 phone survey that was mostly prison converts. The honest replacement: **23% of converts say doubts have made them consider leaving (Yaqeen 2025)**, and in 2001 only about half of new converts were still regular mosque participants.
7. **the reviewing imam's book was not identified.** Shortlist in section 9. He trained at Qalam Seminary (where Sh. Mikaeel Smith was one of his teachers) and in ifta at Al-Salam Institute, which suggests a traditional, likely Hanafi background with multi-madhab training. That matters for the default prayer method (section 8).

**Added in the re-evaluation (§14):**
8. **Family is the most-discussed problem on Reddit**, above loneliness, and *Welcome to Islam* puts telling family on Day 1. Family moves into week 1 and recurs through all three phases.
9. **Two pain points nobody covers:** praying in a non-Muslim home (0 mentions across every book read in full) and the fear that a low-iman patch means you'll leave Islam. These are the clearest openings for our [[Positioning - Pain-Point Curriculum]].
10. **The *Field Guide* is the closest competitor in content** (family letter, burnout, first masjid visit, the few-weeks-in slump), but it's a book read once with no daily sequence, no step-by-step prayer and no masjid layer.

**Recommendation for program length:** a **90-day program in three 30-day phases**, with a 5-minute daily lesson, a calendar-triggered Ramadan/Eid module, and Days 1–7 as the "minimum viable practice" week. Reasoning in section 4.

## 4. Length analysis and recommended program length

**What exists.** Structured programs cluster at **8–14 weekly sessions (2–3.5 months)**: Embrace Islam 101 (8), iERA and Embrace Step by Step (14), SeekersGuidance (14 lessons), Rabata (9). Daily-format series run **30 days** (Faith Revival; Oduro's series is ~30 short episodes). Long tracks exist (ICNA NY 2 years; Darul Arqam INSTIL 6 months; newmuslims.com 10 levels) but are the exception. The retention literature frames risk around the **first 90 days to the first year** (Yaqeen 2025; 2001 study: about half of converts still regular participants after a year).

**Three options.**

| Option | Shape | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. 30 days | One month of daily lessons, then "graduate" to the masjid | Matches the proven daily format; fastest to build; easy to pitch | Ends before the risk window closes; nothing to keep the convert on the app in month 2–3 when loneliness peaks |
| **B. 90 days in three 30-day phases (recommended)** | Phase 1 Foundations (days 1–30) · Phase 2 Belief and worship deepened (31–60) · Phase 3 Living it (61–90); plus a Ramadan/Eid module that fires by calendar whatever day the person is on | Matches program lengths in the market (12 weeks ≈ 8–14 sessions) *and* the 90-day risk window; each phase is a 30-day daily series the format evidence supports; Days 1–7 can ship for the pilot while the rest is written | More content to write and review; needs a "welcome back" design for gaps |
| C. 1 year | 365 lessons or 52 weekly modules | Covers the full first-year risk window | Far too much to write and get reviewed; weekly cadence shows worse retention; no source runs this long except ICNA NY's 2-year program, which has no public syllabus |

**Why B.** It's the only option that fits all three pieces of evidence at once: the daily 5-minute unit (format), the 8–14 week length masjids already recognize (market), and the 90-day social risk window (retention). It also degrades gracefully: a convert who finishes only Phase 1 still has the minimum viable practice.

**Lesson unit:** ≤5 minutes; one thing to learn, one thing to do, one line of encouragement. Template options from the research: Yaqeen's who/what/why/when/where, or Masturah Stewart's four strands (memorize · do · believe · live). Short recall checks and "can you now recite X?" skill checks are rare in the market (only newmuslims.com and Darul Arqam) and worth borrowing.

**Commonly missing in existing curricula:** mental health and the faith dip after conversion; holidays with non-Muslim family (Christmas, Thanksgiving); US workplace and school issues; finding a mentor; being overwhelmed by the community; debt and interest; converts with existing non-Muslim spouses; any measured outcomes.

**Who converts are** (ISPU 2022, Yaqeen 2025): roughly half Black, 17% Hispanic, a quarter white; many lower-income; average age at conversion ~25. So: mobile-first, low data, free to the convert, Spanish-ready later, and examples that don't default to South Asian or Arab culture.

## 6. Where sources disagree (not smoothed over)

**Between curricula**
- **Belief first vs. practice first.** Creed-first (Tarsin, Zarabozo, Bahammam, iERA/Embrace) vs. practice-early (Revealed Path, newmuslims.com, Umar, WhyIslam, SFM). Scholars say all five prayers are due from day one, so practice can't wait 7 weeks; but every source still opens with the shahada's meaning. The resolution the evidence supports: shahada and "who Allah is" on days 1–2, wudu and a minimum prayer by day 3–4, detailed six articles in Phase 2.
- **Where social topics go.** Early (newmuslims.com, Field Guide, Sajid Umar) vs. late or absent (most). The retention data favors early.
- **How to handle madhab.** Most US books present one view without naming a school; Tarsin notes differences; The Revealed Path ships separate worship manuals per school. See section 8.

**Between our own research agents (flagged for you and the scholars)**
- **"Read from a card" during prayer.** Agent D's convert-voice synthesis recommends telling converts it's fine to read from a card or phone. Agent E found the Hanafi view holds that holding a text in prayer is prohibitively disliked and can invalidate it; Shafi'i and Hanbali permit it, and phones are treated like a mushaf (IslamQA 108242; SeekersGuidance 35467). **Since the reviewing imam is Qalam-trained (likely Hanafi), the app's "prayer card" feature needs his ruling before it ships.** The safe default across all schools is the dhikr substitute (IslamQA 5410) or a short memorized verse, with the card used *before and after* prayer to learn.
- **"Start with one prayer today."** Agent D proposed a gentle welcome-back message in those words. Agent E found Tier-1 sources across leanings (IslamWeb 529861; SeekersGuidance 167692) reject presenting fewer than five prayers as allowed, though IslamWeb adds that praying two while acknowledging five is better than none. **Wording must encourage without implying a concession.** Scholar question.
- **What a convert does before learning the prayer.** *Welcome to Islam* says that until you learn the prayer, sitting and thanking Allah at each prayer time "will be your prayer." SeekersGuidance 167692 and IslamWeb 529861 say pray all five with the bare minimum of actions from day one. **Our lesson follows the Tier-1 view pending the scholar (Q11).**
- **Gradualism's limits.** Sources agree gradualism applies to *teaching order and pastoral patience*, not to cancelling obligations. They differ on how far tolerance stretches (al-Qaradawi on hijab vs. IslamWeb).

**Within the religious content** (full map in [[Research Notes - Core Essentials and Madhab Map]], all recorded with both sides, none resolved):

## 8. Madhab approach (your decision, with the evidence)

**Decision logged 2026-10-05:** mainstream Sunni; a short, curiosity-sparking overview of the four schools; practical lessons teach the common valid practice and note differences briefly; madhab is not a focus.

**What the research adds:**
- All four schools regard each other as valid; differences in the prayer don't invalidate it; all accept praying behind an imam of another school (SeekersGuidance; AMJA 78081). That makes the "common practice, note the difference" approach defensible.
- Scholars' advice to converts on choosing a school ranges from *obligatory to follow one* (Murabtal Haaj via Hamza Yusuf; Darul Ifta Birmingham; Yaqeen's Hamdeh) through *strongly recommended, choose on practical grounds* (SeekersGuidance: one you can learn, get answers in, that fits your community) to *not obligatory; follow a trusted local scholar* (ECFR; IslamQA.info; Yasir Qadhi, 2013 London lecture). **Common ground across all three:** don't derive rulings yourself; learn from an accessible qualified teacher; don't let it cause conflict at the masjid. That common ground is what the curriculum should say.
- **The practical question is the default prayer method**, since hand position, raising the hands, basmala, Fatiha behind the imam and the tashahhud finger all differ. Two workable models: (a) *The Revealed Path* model: one curriculum with a per-school worship track chosen at onboarding (or by the masjid admin); (b) one default set by the pilot masjid, with a one-line "other schools do X" note. **Recommendation: (b) for the pilot, designed so (a) is a data field later** (masjid → default school). the reviewing imam sets the the pilot masjid default.
- Week-one differences that affect converts most (full table in the notes): touching the opposite sex and wudu (matters for healthcare and service jobs), bleeding and wudu, socks, Asr time, meat.
